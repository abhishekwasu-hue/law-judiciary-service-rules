# 40-G  Case-law: Disciplinary proceedings, suspension, sealed cover, judicial review, time-limits

**Scope**: Court staff (Group A–D) challenge suspension orders, charge-sheets, enquiry reports, penalties, and denial of promotion under the sealed-cover procedure. Rules: MCS (Discipline & Appeal) Rules 1979 (Rr. 4 suspension, 8 major-penalty enquiry, 10 minor penalties; Art. 311), GAD GRs on DE time-limits and sealed cover (library files 51–52). For High Court staff, the disciplinary authority is the Chief Justice/Registrar General under the High Court Rules (Art. 229); for district court staff, the District Judge — the State is usually a formal party.

## 2. Case details

### 2.1 Tulsiram Patel (1985) ✖
- Constitution Bench on Art. 311(2) second proviso and the parallel rule (MCS D&A Rules 1979 — rule corresponding to CCS (CCA) R. 19). Dismissal/removal/reduction without enquiry in the three situations is constitutional; the authority must record its satisfaction (for clause (b)) that it is not reasonably practicable to hold the enquiry; the satisfaction is subject to limited judicial review; the departmental appeal is the forum to re-examine.
- **Use**: court staff convicted of offences (e.g. bribery under PC Act, misappropriation of court deposits) — dismissal under clause (a) without enquiry; State/High Court defends by producing the conviction and the reasoned order; petitioner argues the conviction is for an offence unconnected with duty / sentence stayed on appeal (stay of conviction matters, not of sentence).

### 2.2 B. Karunakar (1993) ✖; B.C. Chaturvedi (1995) ✖; Kuldeep Singh (1999) ✖; P. Gunasekaran (2014) ✖
- Karunakar: supply of the enquiry report before the penalty order; if not supplied, court must see prejudice; the remedy is to remit from that stage, not reinstatement with back wages.
- Chaturvedi (standard quotation, verify from text): the disciplinary authority is the sole judge of facts; "the court/tribunal in its power of judicial review does not act as an appellate authority to re-appreciate the evidence"; interference where there is no evidence, or the conclusion is perverse, or natural justice violated; on proportionality, the court may interfere only if the punishment "shocks the conscience", and should normally remit to the authority to reconsider the penalty.
- Kuldeep Singh: where the finding rests on no evidence / is perverse, the court can interfere — the "no evidence" rule.
- Gunasekaran: list of prohibited areas of High Court review under Art. 226 (re-appreciating evidence, acting as appellate authority, going into adequacy/reliability, correcting errors of fact, interfering where some evidence exists, etc.).
- **State uses** (as formal respondent supporting the High Court's/District Judge's order): limited scope of review, no reappreciation, remit rather than substitute. **Petitioners use** Kuldeep Singh ("no evidence") and Karunakar (report not supplied; prejudice).

### 2.3 K.V. Jankiraman (1991) ✖ — sealed cover
- Sealed cover is justified only when, on the date of DPC, a charge-sheet in a DE or criminal case has been issued or the employee is under suspension; not for pending preliminary enquiry/vigilance investigation. On full exoneration, the sealed cover is opened and the employee promoted from the date his juniors were, with arrears of pay at discretion (ordinarily full arrears if exonerated; the Court also said the authority may deny back-pay in given circumstances). On a minor penalty, DPC's recommendation may be acted upon from the date of penalty (per DoPT OMs, which Maharashtra GAD follows — library file 52).
- **Use**: court staff DPCs for Senior Clerk/Assistant Superintendent/Stenographer grades; petitioners attack sealed cover where only a "show-cause" or preliminary enquiry was pending; State/High Court must show the charge-sheet date vs DPC date.

### 2.4 Ajay Kumar Choudhary (SC, 16.02.2015) ✔ — suspension
- Source: https://hcraj.nic.in/sci/2015/3-1912-2015_2015-02-16.pdf (C.A. 1912/2015).
- **Verbatim ¶14**: "We, therefore, direct that the currency of a Suspension Order should not extend beyond three months if within this period the Memorandum of Charges/ Chargesheet is not served on the delinquent officer/ employee"; if served, a reasoned order may extend suspension. DoPT implemented it by OM 03.07.2015 / 23.08.2016 (review of suspension under CCS (CCA) R. 10). Maharashtra GAD has issued corresponding instructions on review of suspension (90 days; review committee) — library file 52 (verify GR number).
- **Petitioners use**: revocation of suspension after 3 months without charge-sheet; subsistence allowance enhancement. **State/High Court use**: where charge-sheet was served within 3 months and a reasoned review extended suspension, Choudhary does not require revocation; suspension is not a punishment (Art. 311 not attracted); the review committee's decision is the operative order.

### 2.5 Prem Nath Bali v. Registrar, High Court of Delhi (SC, 16.12.2015) ✖
- A Delhi High Court employee under suspension for years while DE dragged on. SC held: the employer should make every effort to conclude the DE within **six months**, extendable for recorded reasons up to a maximum of one year; prolonged DE and suspension cause hardship and are contrary to public interest; directions on subsistence allowance/treatment of suspension period. (Open the text before quoting the paragraph.)
- **Use**: petitioners (court staff) seek quashing of DE or full pay for the suspension period on the ground of delay. State/High Court: delay attributable to the employee's non-cooperation/adjournments; delay alone does not vitiate unless prejudice; GAD GR timelines are directory.

### 2.6 Ajit Kumar Nag (2005) ✖; B.K. Meena (1996) ✖; Paul Anthony (1999) ✖
- Simultaneous DE and criminal trial permissible; stay of DE is the exception (grave charges, identical evidence, prejudice); acquittal in the criminal case does not automatically entitle reinstatement unless the acquittal is honourable and the DE was on identical evidence (Paul Anthony). **Use**: court staff facing ACB trap cases — DE continues; petitioner seeks stay pending trial.

### 2.7 SBI v. Ajay Kumar Sood (SC, 16.08.2022) ✔
- Source: LiveLaw PDF of the judgment (C.A. 5305/2022). Disciplinary dismissal was converted to compulsory retirement by CGIT; HC affirmed in an unreasoned, incomprehensible judgment. **Verbatim ¶11**: "The judgment of the High Court is simply incomprehensible leaving this Court with no option than to remand the proceedings." **¶10**: "The purpose of judicial writing is not to confuse or confound the reader behind the veneer of complex language." The Court laid down judgment-writing guidance (structure, paragraph numbering, accessibility, digital signing).
- **Use**: both sides invoke it for the duty to give reasons — disciplinary/appellate authorities must record reasons when disagreeing with the enquiry officer or when rejecting an appeal (see also *Ramadhar Sao* 2025 ◐: detailed reasons not needed when the enquiry report is accepted).

### 2.8 Upendra Singh (1994) ✖
- No interference at the charge-sheet stage; the truth of the charges is for the enquiry. **State/High Court use**: when court staff rush to the High Court against a charge-sheet or show-cause notice.

## 3. Drafting checklist — disciplinary replies
1. Identify the disciplinary/appellate authority (Chief Justice/Registrar General/District Judge) and the rule applied; State to adopt their affidavit on facts.
2. Suspension: date of suspension, date of charge-sheet, review committee dates (Ajay Kumar Choudhary ¶14; GAD GR).
3. DE timeline: dates of charge-sheet, EO appointment, hearings, adjournments sought by the employee, report, show-cause, penalty (Prem Nath Bali).
4. Natural justice: report supplied? reply considered? reasons for disagreement recorded? (Karunakar; Sood).
5. Scope of review: Chaturvedi/Gunasekaran; proportionality — Dipak Mali/Vasanthi line (remit, not substitute).
6. Sealed cover: charge-sheet/suspension status on the DPC date (Jankiraman).
7. Criminal case: stage; identical facts? (B.K. Meena; Paul Anthony; Ajit Kumar Nag).

## 4. मराठी सारांश
- *तुलसीराम पटेल*: अनु. 311(2) च्या दुसऱ्या परंतुकानुसार दोषसिद्धी, चौकशी व्यवहार्य नसणे (कारणे नोंदवून) किंवा राज्याची सुरक्षा — या तीन स्थितींत चौकशीशिवाय बडतर्फी वैध.
- न्यायिक पुनर्विलोकनाची मर्यादा (*बी.सी. चतुर्वेदी*, *गुणशेखरन*): न्यायालय पुराव्याचे पुनर्मूल्यांकन करत नाही; "पुरावाच नाही" (*कुलदीप सिंग*), नैसर्गिक न्यायाचा भंग किंवा धक्कादायक विषम शिक्षा असेल तरच हस्तक्षेप — आणि तोही प्रकरण परत पाठवून, शिक्षा स्वतः न बदलता (*दीपक माली*, *वसंती*).
- निलंबन: 3 महिन्यांत दोषारोपपत्र न दिल्यास निलंबन चालू ठेवता येत नाही (*अजय कुमार चौधरी* ¶14); विभागीय चौकशी 6 महिन्यांत, जास्तीत जास्त 1 वर्ष (*प्रेमनाथ बाली* — दिल्ली उच्च न्यायालय कर्मचाऱ्याचे प्रकरण).
- बंद लिफाफा फक्त दोषारोपपत्र/निलंबन DPC च्या तारखेला प्रलंबित असेल तरच (*जानकीरामन*); फौजदारी व विभागीय कारवाई एकाच वेळी चालू शकते (*अजित कुमार नाग*, *बी.के. मीणा*).
- आदेशांत कारणे आवश्यक (*अजय कुमार सूद*); चौकशी अहवाल मान्य करताना सविस्तर कारणे नकोत, असहमतीवेळी हवीत (*रामाधार साव* 2025).
