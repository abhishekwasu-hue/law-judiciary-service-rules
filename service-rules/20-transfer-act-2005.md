### Structure
- Chapter I — Preliminary (ss. 1–2)
- **Chapter II — "TENURES OF POSTING AND TRANSFER AND TRANSFERRING AUTHORITY"** (ss. 3–7)
- **Chapter III — "PREVENTION OF DELAY IN DISCHARGE OF OFFICIAL DUTIES"** (ss. 8–12)
- Chapter IV — Miscellaneous (ss. 13–16: rules, repeal etc.)

### Key provisions (close paraphrase of fetched text; short verbatim quotes marked)
**Section 1(3) — application.** The Act applies to all Government servants in State services including AIS officers of the Maharashtra cadre. Proviso: Chapter II "shall not apply to the employees appointed on non-transferable posts in isolated cadres"; Chapter II also does not apply to **employees under the administrative control of the Judiciary**, and (by 2014 amendment) to the Police Force / IPS officers.
**Section 2(f) — "Government servant"** includes employees under the administrative control of the Judiciary (but not judicial officers). Net effect for court staff: **Chapter III (delay) applies; Chapter II (tenure/transfer) does not.** Transfers of court staff are governed by the High Court's own rules/circulars under Article 229 / Article 235.

**Section 3 — Tenure of posting.** Normal tenure in a post: **three years** for AIS officers and Group A, B and C employees. Group C non-secretariat employees must be moved after two full tenures in the same office/department; secretariat employees not more than three years in a post and not more than two consecutive tenures in one department. **Group D:** normally no fixed tenure; transfer out of station only on request against a clear vacancy, mutual transfer, or on a substantiated serious complaint.

**Section 4 — Tenure of transfer.** No transfer before completion of tenure except as provided. Competent authorities prepare annual transfer lists; transfers ordinarily **once a year in April–May**. **s. 4(4):** mid-year transfers allowed (i) to newly created posts or vacancies arising from retirement, promotion, resignation, reversion, reinstatement, consequential vacancy or return from leave; (ii) where the competent authority finds the transfer essential due to exceptional circumstances or special reasons, recorded in writing, with "prior approval of the next higher authority". **s. 4(5):** in special cases, with reasons recorded in writing and prior approval of the immediately superior transferring authority under the s. 6 table, an employee may be transferred before completing tenure.

**Section 5 — Extension of tenure** in three situations: less than one year to retirement; special technical qualification with no ready replacement; nearly completed project. **Not more than 30%** of employees in any office/department to be transferred in a year.

**Section 6 — Transferring authority** (table): Chief Minister for senior Group A; Minister-in-charge / Secretary for other Group A and Group B; Heads of Departments for Group B and C; Regional Heads for Group D; powers may be delegated.

**Section 7** — each administrative department to publish the list of Heads of Departments, Regional Heads and competent transferring authorities.

**Section 8 — Citizens' Charter** with time limits; failure to decide within time limits leads to fixing of responsibility. **Section 9 — delegation of powers** and maximum three levels of submission; lists updated every 1 April.

**Section 10 — Prevention of delay.** (1) Duties to be discharged diligently and expeditiously; a file normally not to remain pending with one employee for more than **seven working days**; "immediate" files same day / next morning; "urgent" within **four days**; matters needing no other department's consultation decided within **45 days**, those needing it within **three months**. (2) Wilful or intentional delay or negligence is dereliction of duty attracting disciplinary action under AIS (D&A) Rules 1969 / **Maharashtra Civil Services (Discipline and Appeal) Rules, 1979**. (3) Competent authority to take action and record an entry in the ACR.
**Section 11** — s. 10 does not apply to sub-judice matters, Lokayukta/Upa-Lokayukta cases, quasi-judicial matters, Centre/other-State matters, legislation and major policy decisions. **Section 12** — administrative audit mechanism within 12 months. **Section 14** — rule-making power. **Section 16** — repeal of Ordinance of 2003.

### How it is used in litigation
- **Petitioner's claims (court staff):** transfer before completion of 3-year tenure; transfer in violation of s. 4(4)/(5) without reasons / without higher approval; mid-term transfer as punishment; "30% ceiling" breached; request-transfer refused.
- **State's / High Court's usual stand:** **Chapter II does not apply to employees under the administrative control of the Judiciary (s. 1(3) proviso)**, so ss. 3–7 cannot be invoked by court staff; transfer is an incident of service; the High Court's administrative rules/circulars govern; no vested right to a posting; scope of judicial review limited to mala fides/ violation of statutory rules (State of U.P. v. Gobardhan Lal line). Where Chapter II does apply (Mantralaya/ L&J Dept staff), the State pleads that the transfer was made with recorded reasons and higher approval under s. 4(4)(ii)/4(5), or against a clear vacancy under s. 4(4)(i).
- Chapter III (s. 10 timelines) is sometimes cited by petitioners complaining of delay in deciding representations/pension; the State replies that s. 11 excludes sub-judice/quasi-judicial matters and that s. 10 creates internal accountability, not an enforceable right to a particular decision.
