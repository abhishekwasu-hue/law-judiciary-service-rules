# 40-F  Case-law: Compassionate appointment

**Scope**: Petitions by heirs of deceased court staff (Group C/D) against the High Court/District Judge and the State: delay in appointment from the waiting list, rejection on income/indigence, age crossed while waiting, substitution of the nominee, demand for Group C instead of Group D, applicability of GAD GR 21.09.2017 (as amended 2019) vs the revised policy GR 17.07.2025 (library file 36). The Supreme Court line from *Umesh Kumar Nagpal* (1994) to *Canara Bank v. Ajithkumar* (2025) is the State's main armour; Bombay HC decisions on substitution/waiting list are the petitioners'.

**Verification key**: ✔ official text opened; ◐ secondary source only — *citation from secondary source — verify*; ✖ standard reporter — *verify*.

## 2. Case details

### 2.1 Umesh Kumar Nagpal v. State of Haryana (SC, 1994) ✖
- Core propositions (paraphrase; open text before quoting): (i) compassionate appointment is an exception to the rule of open recruitment, permissible only to meet the immediate hardship of the family; (ii) it is not a vested right nor heritable; (iii) only to posts of the lowest categories (Class III/IV), never to higher posts; (iv) the object is to tide over the sudden crisis, so it cannot be claimed after a lapse of time or when the family has survived; (v) the policy's terms govern; (vi) the authority must examine the financial condition.
- **State uses**: foundational citation in every reply — denial for delayed applications, demand for a Group C post when only Group D is offered, claims by married daughters/grandchildren beyond the policy, claims after the family received substantial terminal benefits.

### 2.2 SAIL v. Madhusudan Das (SC, 2008) ✖
- The applicant was a minor when his father died; applied years later. Held: compassionate appointment must be granted strictly under the scheme in force; sympathy cannot supply what the scheme does not; the purpose is immediate relief, and a claim years later defeats it. HC direction to appoint set aside.
- **Use**: against "minor at the time of death — apply on attaining majority" claims (now also Tinku, 2024).

### 2.3 State of H.P. v. Shashi Kumar (SC, 16.01.2019) ✔
- Source: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2016/6511/6511_2016_Judgement_16-Jan-2019.pdf (C.A. 988/2019).
- Verbatim (page refs, judgment has no printed para numbers): "it is a well-settled principle of law that there is no right to compassionate appointment" (p.17); "The Policy does not preclude the dependants of a deceased employee" from consideration merely because they receive family pension — "What the Policy mandates is that the receipt of family pension should be taken into account" in assessing indigence (p.18); "the basis of a scheme of compassionate appointment lies in the need of providing immediate assistance" (p.30). Income limits upheld; State directed to revise them periodically (preferably every three years). Claim rejected also for delay (application 2007, writ 2015).
- **State uses**: (a) family pension/gratuity/GIS/leave encashment are legitimately counted in the income test under the Maharashtra GR; (b) income limit in the GR is valid; (c) delay. **Petitioners use**: the direction to revise income limits periodically — argue the Maharashtra limit is outdated.

### 2.4 Central Coalfields v. Parden Oraon (SC, 09.04.2021) ✔
- Source: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2020/17121/17121_2020_38_1502_27432_Judgement_09-Apr-2021.pdf (C.A. 897/2021).
- **Verbatim ¶8**: "The whole object of granting compassionate appointment is to enable the family to tide over the sudden crisis" … "compassionate employment cannot be granted after a lapse of reasonable period" … "compassionate appointment cannot be claimed or offered after a signficant lapse of time" (sic). **¶9**: "As the object of compassionate appointment is for providing immediate succour to the family of a deceased employee" … "the Respondent's son is not entitled for compassionate appointment after the passage of a long period of time"; application was filed in 2013, "more than 10 years after the Respondent's husband had gone missing". Also ¶9: "There is no bar in the National Coal Wage Agreement for appointment of the son of an employee who has suffered civil death" — relief nevertheless refused for delay and absence of financial crisis.
- **State uses**: against applications/petitions filed many years after death; against claims after the family has managed for years (shows no "crisis").

### 2.5 N.C. Santhosh (2020) ✖ / Canara Bank v. Ajithkumar (SC, 11.02.2025) ✔ — which scheme applies?
- Santhosh (three-judge): norms prevailing on the date of consideration apply. Other three-judge decisions apply the scheme at the date of death. *Ajithkumar* ¶15–16, 21–22 records the conflict and leaves it to a larger bench (LiveLaw 2024 report also noted a reference).
- Ajithkumar source: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2019/45084/45084_2019_14_1502_59243_Judgement_11-Feb-2025.pdf (C.A. 255/2025). **Verbatim**: ¶11(i) "None can claim compassionate appointment, on the occurrence of death/medical incapacitation of the concerned employee"; ¶11(j) "An application for compassionate appointment has to be made immediately upon death/incapacitation"; ¶29 "It is only in 'hand-to-mouth' cases that a claim for compassionate appointment ought to be considered and granted" … "This has to be distinguished from a mere fall in standard of life arising out of the death of the bread earner."; ¶26 "such relief may not be denied solely on account of time lost in prosecuting proceedings"; ¶27 "Lapse of time could, however, be a major factor for denying compassionate appointment where the claim is lodged belatedly."
- **Maharashtra application**: GR 17.07.2025 superseded GR 21.09.2017 in material part (library file 36). The State's stand: applications pending/considered after 17.07.2025 are governed by the 2025 policy (Santhosh); petitioners whose relative died earlier argue the 2017 GR (e.g. Group D appointee's priority for Group C — clause 16, dropped in 2025) should apply — cite the pending larger-bench reference and *Ajithkumar* ¶26–28 (litigation delay not to be counted against them).
- **State uses ¶29** ("hand-to-mouth") to justify income-based rejection; **petitioners use ¶26–28** where the office sat on the application.

### 2.6 Tinku v. State of Haryana (SC, 14.11.2024) ◐ and State of W.B. v. Debabrata Tiwari (2023) ✖
- Tinku: applicant was 7 at father's death (1997); claim after majority (2005) rejected — the policy at the relevant time barred minors and the family had survived; no vested right. Debabrata Tiwari: SC set aside Calcutta HC directions for appointments decades after death; authorities must decide applications promptly, but delay by the authority does not create a right where the crisis has passed.

### 2.7 Bombay HC decisions on the Maharashtra GR 21.09.2017 ◐ (verify neutral citations)
- **Substitution of nominee**: Nagpur DB (*Kalpana*, 2024:BHC-NAG:5826-DB) and a Principal-seat decision reported by LiveLaw (2024) allowed a son to be substituted for the widow who was nearing the age limit on the waiting list, reading the GR's prohibition on change of nominee as arbitrary/defeating the object. **State's answer**: GR 17.07.2025 now governs substitution (check clause); the policy may limit substitution to one change within a time limit; the waiting list cannot be re-ranked.
- **Age crossed on waiting list**: petitioners argue the age bar should be relaxed for the period on the waiting list (several Bombay HC orders directing relaxation — verify). State: the GR's age limit (45) and 20% quota/waiting list operation (seniority by date of forwarding) are policy; relaxation only as the GR permits.
- **Two-children norm; delayed application**: Bombay HC upheld rejection where the deceased had more than two children (small-family norm under the Maharashtra Civil Services (Declaration of Small Family) Rules 2005) and where the son applied years late (SCC Online blog, 2024).
- **Madhuri Vidhate (SC, 2022)** ✖: SC set aside a Bombay HC direction to appoint contrary to the Maharashtra scheme — the State's go-to citation when the High Court is inclined to direct appointment "in the interest of justice".

## 3. Drafting checklist — compassionate appointment replies (court establishments)
1. Date of death; date of application; date of forwarding by the District Judge/Registrar; waiting-list seniority; any vacancy in Group D/C (20% quota).
2. Financial condition: family pension, gratuity, GIS, leave encashment, other earning members, property — Shashi Kumar / Ajithkumar ¶29.
3. Policy applicable (2017 GR vs 2025 GR; Santhosh vs date-of-death — reference pending); clause on substitution/age/time limit.
4. Delay: Parden Oraon ¶8–9; Nagpal; but concede Ajithkumar ¶26 where the office delayed.
5. Appointing authority is the High Court/District Judge — State's role: policy and sanction of posts; avoid admissions on behalf of the High Court administration.

## 4. मराठी सारांश
- अनुकंपा नियुक्ती हा अनु. 14/16 ला अपवाद — कुटुंबावर कोसळलेले **तात्काळ संकट** निवारण्यापुरता; तो वारसाहक्क नाही, फक्त खालच्या पदांवर, धोरणाच्या अटींनुसारच (*उमेश नागपाल*, *मधुसूदन दास*).
- *शशी कुमार* (2019): अनुकंपा नियुक्तीचा हक्क नाही; कुटुंबनिवृत्तिवेतन/उपदान उत्पन्नात धरणे योग्य; उत्पन्न मर्यादा वैध पण वेळोवेळी सुधारावी; विलंब घातक.
- *परडेन ओराव* (2021) ¶8–9: "वाजवी कालावधीनंतर" नियुक्ती नाही — 10 वर्षांनंतरचा अर्ज फेटाळला. *कॅनरा बँक वि. अजितकुमार* (2025): फक्त "हातावर पोट" असलेल्या कुटुंबांनाच (¶29); पण कार्यालय/न्यायालयीन विलंब अर्जदाराविरुद्ध धरू नये (¶26–28); "मृत्यूची तारीख की विचाराची तारीख" हा प्रश्न मोठ्या पीठाकडे.
- महाराष्ट्र: शा.नि. 21.09.2017 (सुधारणा 2019) → शा.नि. 17.07.2025; मुंबई उच्च न्यायालयाने प्रतीक्षायादीतील नामनिर्देशित व्यक्ती बदलण्यास (विधवा → मुलगा) परवानगी दिली (*कल्पना* 2024 नागपूर इ.); दोन अपत्यांचा निकष व उशिरा अर्ज फेटाळणे मान्य केले.
- उत्तरात: मृत्यू/अर्ज/अग्रेषण तारखा, आर्थिक स्थिती, लागू धोरण, रिक्त पदे व प्रतीक्षायादीतील क्रम स्पष्ट करावा; नियुक्ती प्राधिकारी उच्च न्यायालय/जिल्हा न्यायाधीश असल्याचे नमूद करावे.
