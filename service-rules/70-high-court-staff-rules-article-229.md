# Bombay High Court staff — rules framed by the Chief Justice under Article 229 (Appellate Side / Original Side / Goa establishment)
## मुंबई उच्च न्यायालयाचे अधिकारी व कर्मचारी — अनुच्छेद २२९ अन्वये मुख्य न्यायमूर्तींनी केलेले नियम

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Constitutional basis | **Article 229(1)–(2)**: appointments of officers and servants of a High Court are made by the Chief Justice (or a Judge/officer directed by him); conditions of service are prescribed by rules made by the Chief Justice, subject, so far as they relate to salaries, allowances, leave or pensions, to the **approval of the Governor**. Article 229(3): administrative expenses charged on the Consolidated Fund of the State. |

### What the fetched material establishes
1. Recruitment to High Court posts (including Law Clerks on contract at ₹40,000 consolidated, one-year term, age 21–30, selection by a committee of Judges nominated by the Chief Justice) is made **"by order of Honourable the Chief Justice"** through the Registrar General — illustrating the Article 229 scheme: the State is not the appointing authority.
2. Rules on salaries/allowances/leave/pension need the Governor's approval (Art. 229(2) proviso); Government (Law & Judiciary Dept / Finance Dept) therefore becomes a necessary party in pay/pension matters of High Court staff, while recruitment, promotion, seniority, transfer and discipline are the Chief Justice's domain.
3. The Supreme Court in the Goa matter treats departure by the State from the Chief Justice's draft rules as impermissible — persuasive for Maharashtra: Government cannot unilaterally alter High Court staff service rules.

### How it is used in litigation
- **Petitioner's claims (HC staff):** pay parity with Mantralaya / Supreme Court staff; higher pay scale recommended by the Chief Justice but not approved by Government; promotion/seniority under HC rules; regularisation of contract staff (e.g. Law Clerks, DEOs); pension benefits for Goa-bench retirees on Maharashtra pattern.
- **State's usual stand:** under Article 229 the Chief Justice is the appointing/disciplinary authority and the State has no role in recruitment, promotion, seniority or transfer — the High Court (Registrar General) is the proper respondent; Government's role is confined to approval under the proviso to Art. 229(2) for salaries/allowances/leave/pension and to budget (Art. 229(3)); approval is a policy decision with financial implications and parity cannot be claimed as of right (equation of posts is for expert bodies — Pay Commission); where the Chief Justice's proposal has been forwarded, the State records the stage of consideration.

- अनुच्छेद २२९: उच्च न्यायालयाच्या अधिकारी/कर्मचाऱ्यांच्या नियुक्त्या मुख्य न्यायमूर्ती करतात; सेवाशर्तींचे नियम मुख्य न्यायमूर्ती करतात — वेतन, भत्ते, रजा व निवृत्तिवेतनासंबंधी नियमांना राज्यपालांची मान्यता आवश्यक.
- मुंबई उच्च न्यायालयाच्या अपील शाखा / मूळ शाखा कर्मचारी नियमांचे अधिकृत मजकूर/शीर्षक bombayhighcourt.nic.in वर सापडले नाही (not verified); गोवा आस्थापनेसाठी २०२३ चे नियम सर्वोच्च न्यायालयात (W.P.(C) 464/2023) वादग्रस्त.
- शासनाची भूमिका: भरती, पदोन्नती, ज्येष्ठता, बदली हे मुख्य न्यायमूर्तींचे क्षेत्र; शासन फक्त वेतन/निवृत्तिवेतन मंजुरी व अर्थसंकल्पापुरते.
