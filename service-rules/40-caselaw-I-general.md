# 40-I  Case-law: General principles — Art. 14/16 parity, equal pay for equal work, Art. 309 rules vs executive instructions, retrospective rules, judgment in rem vs in personam (extension to non-litigants), negative equality

**Scope**: Cross-cutting doctrines pleaded in almost every service writ by court staff — "my colleague got it, so must I"; "same work as the Secretariat/High Court clerk, so same pay"; "the GR cannot override the Rules"; "the amendment cannot take away my accrued right"; "the Bombay HC judgment in X's case binds the State for all of us"; and the State's counter-doctrines.

## 2. Principles and how they are used

### 2.1 Equal pay for equal work — Jagjit Singh (2016) ✖; Chaurasia (1989) ✖; Dibyendu Bhattacharya (2011) ✖; Charanjit Singh (2006) ✖
- Jagjit Singh (Khehar J): after surveying the law, held that temporary employees (daily-wage, ad-hoc, casual, contract) who perform the same duties as regular employees are entitled to the minimum of the regular pay scale; denial is "exploitative enslavement" of the vulnerable. Conditions: identical duties/responsibilities, same qualifications, same hierarchy; not for a different post/ cadre; not for increments/allowances unless rules provide.
- Chaurasia / Dibyendu Bhattacharya / Charanjit Singh: equal pay is not mechanical; pay is for expert bodies; different recruitment mode/qualification/responsibility justifies different scales; burden on the claimant; courts do not sit as pay commissions.
- **Petitioners (court staff)**: outsourced/daily-wage sweepers, drivers, data-entry operators, Court Managers claim the minimum of the regular scale (Jagjit Singh); Small Causes/City Civil Court clerks claim parity with High Court/Secretariat clerks or with district-court clerks after Shetty Commission.
- **State**: (i) Jagjit Singh needs proof of identical work on the same post in the same establishment — show the recruitment rules, qualifications and duties differ; (ii) parity across establishments (HC vs district courts vs Secretariat) is barred by Art. 229 / separate rule-making and by Chaurasia (pay commissions decide); (iii) *Court Stenographers* (2002) — pay cannot be fixed by writ for HC staff (file H); (iv) negative equality — a wrong scale to one person is no basis.

### 2.2 Negative equality — Jai Singh (2003) ✖, Ram Kumar Mann (1997) ✖, Chandigarh Admn v. Jagjit Singh (1995) ✖
- Standard proposition (Chandigarh Admn, para 8 — verify): "if an illegality or irregularity has been committed in favour of any individual… others cannot invoke the jurisdiction of the High Court… seeking a direction to repeat or perpetuate such illegality"; Art. 14 is a positive concept and cannot be used to claim a wrong benefit.
- **State uses**: when the petitioner relies on an erroneous pay fixation / promotion / increment / OPS option granted to a colleague (often by a different District Court or a different Treasury), or on an interim order in another's case. Pair with Rafiq Masih (no recovery) to show that the wrong benefit is being corrected prospectively, not perpetuated.
- **Petitioners use**: the earlier benefit was *not* illegal but a conscious decision; discrimination between similarly placed employees (Pawan Kumar 2026 — file C).

### 2.3 Art. 309 rules vs executive instructions (GRs/circulars) — Sant Ram Sharma (1967) ✖, K.P. Joseph (1973) ✖, Jitendra Kumar Srivastava (2013) ✔ (file E)
- Statutory rules (MCS Rules under Art. 309 proviso; High Court rules under Art. 229; Pension Rules 1982) prevail over GRs/circulars; GRs can supplement where rules are silent (Sant Ram Sharma) and can confer enforceable benefits (K.P. Joseph) but cannot curtail a right given by the rules or impose a deprivation (Jitendra Srivastava ¶15 — a circular is not "law" for Art. 300A).
- **Petitioners use**: against FD/GAD circulars restricting increments (e.g. GR 26.12.2011 on EOL — file A), restricting ACP eligibility beyond the scheme GR, withholding gratuity "pending audit", or imposing conditions not in the recruitment rules.
- **State uses**: many Maharashtra "rules" (ACP/TBP schemes, DA/HRA, compassionate appointment, NPS) are themselves executive schemes — the GR *is* the source of the benefit, so its conditions bind (K.P. Joseph cuts both ways); where the Rules confer discretion (e.g. Rule 18 of the Revised Pay Rules — FD interprets), the GR is the exercise of that discretion.

### 2.4 Retrospective rules / accrued rights — B.S. Yadav ✖, T.R. Kapur ✖, P.D. Aggarwal ✖, Rangadhamaiah ✖
- Rules under Art. 309 can be retrospective, but (i) cannot take away rights already accrued (seniority earned, promotion already made, pension already earned) arbitrarily; (ii) eligibility changes apply prospectively unless clearly stated; (iii) retrospective reduction of pension is unconstitutional (Rangadhamaiah).
- **Petitioners use**: against amendments to recruitment rules (qualification/MS-CIT/Marathi exam — library files 60–61) applied to those already in service or already in the select list; against GR 17.07.2025 compassionate policy applied to pending cases; against NPS/UPS option conditions.
- **State uses**: a mere chance/expectation (e.g. of promotion under old rules) is not a vested right; rules in force on the date of consideration govern (N.C. Santhosh line; *Deepak Agarwal v. State of U.P.* (2011) 6 SCC 725 — vacancy filled per rules in force when the selection process starts, unless the rules say otherwise).

### 2.5 Judgment in rem vs in personam — Arvind Kumar Srivastava (SC, 17.10.2014) ✔; Amrit Lal Berry ✖; K.I. Shephard ✖; Jaswant Singh ✖
- Source: https://hcraj.nic.in/sci/2014/3-9849-2014_2014-10-17.pdf — **¶23** (summarised; full text longer than can be quoted here): (1) "Normal rule is that when a particular set of employees is given relief by the Court," all identically situated persons should get the same benefit — denial may violate Art. 14; not approaching the court earlier is not by itself a ground to deny; (2) exception — those who slept over their rights and came after others succeeded are "treated as fence-sitters"; delay/laches/acquiescence justify dismissal; (3) the exception does not apply where the judgment was meant for all similarly situated (judgment in rem — typically policy/scheme matters): "the obligation is cast upon the authorities to itself extend the benefit thereof" without litigation; (4) where in personam (expressly or by tenor), others must show their petition "does not suffer from either laches and delays or acquiescence."
- **DoPT stand (Government of India)**: DoPT instructions (e.g. OM in the Mundinamani/Ayyamperumal context, 2021, and the general instruction that a judgment in personam is to be implemented only for the parties, while a judgment in rem/declaring a rule invalid is to be extended generally after Law Ministry advice) — Maharashtra GAD follows the same practice: a court order is implemented for the petitioners; extension to others requires a policy decision (and FD concurrence for financial implications). **Note**: the Supreme Court's *Siddaraj* clarification (file A) is the model of a court itself defining the in-rem reach (third parties from 01.05.2023 only).
- **State uses**: (i) to confine Bombay HC single-petition judgments (e.g. a stenographer's Shetty grade, a 30-June retiree's increment) to the petitioners; (ii) to resist contempt/representations by non-parties; (iii) Jaswant Singh/Arvind Kumar for fence-sitters. **Petitioners use**: ¶23(3) — if the judgment struck down/ read down a GR or declared a rule, it is in rem and the State must extend it suo motu; Art. 14; *Amrit Lal Berry* (don't force everyone to litigate).
- **Drafting**: in replies, analyse the earlier judgment's operative part — did it quash a GR (in rem) or direct relief to named petitioners (in personam)? State the date of the earlier judgment and the petitioner's delay.

## 3. Drafting checklist — general pleas
1. Parity claims: identify post, establishment, recruitment rules, qualification, duties; cite Chaurasia/Charanjit Singh; negative equality if the comparator's benefit was erroneous.
2. Equal pay for temporary staff: Jagjit Singh conditions; nature of engagement (file C).
3. Rules vs GR: identify the statutory rule; if the benefit itself flows from a GR, its conditions bind.
4. Retrospectivity: date of accrual of the right; date of amendment; whether the amendment speaks of pending cases.
5. In rem/in personam: operative part of the earlier judgment; delay; FD concurrence; cite Arvind Kumar Srivastava ¶23 and Siddaraj.

## 4. मराठी सारांश
- समान कामासाठी समान वेतन (*जगजीत सिंग* 2016): तात्पुरते/रोजंदारी कर्मचारी नियमित कर्मचाऱ्यांइतकेच काम करत असल्यास नियमित पदाच्या वेतनश्रेणीचे **किमान वेतन** मिळते; पण ते यांत्रिक तत्त्व नाही — पात्रता, भरतीपद्धत, जबाबदारी वेगळी असल्यास वेगळे वेतन समर्थनीय (*चौरसिया*, *चरणजीत सिंग*, *दिब्येंदू भट्टाचार्य*); वेतनश्रेणी ठरवणे वेतन आयोगाचे काम.
- "नकारात्मक समानता": एखाद्याला चुकून दिलेला लाभ इतरांना मागता येत नाही (*राम कुमार मान*, *चंदीगड प्रशासन वि. जगजीत सिंग*, *जय सिंग* — तथ्ये पडताळावीत).
- अनु. 309 चे नियम शासननिर्णय/परिपत्रकांपेक्षा वरचढ; शासननिर्णय फक्त पोकळी भरू शकतो (*संत राम शर्मा*), पण जिथे लाभच शासननिर्णयातून येतो तिथे त्याच्या अटी बंधनकारक (*के.पी. जोसेफ*).
- नियम पूर्वलक्षी करता येतात, पण मिळालेले हक्क (ज्येष्ठता, निवृत्तिवेतन) काढून घेता येत नाहीत (*बी.एस. यादव*, *टी.आर. कपूर*, *रंगधमैय्या*).
- *अरविंद कुमार श्रीवास्तव* ¶23: निकाल सर्वांसाठी (in rem — धोरण/योजना) असेल तर शासनाने स्वतःहून समान स्थितीतील सर्वांना लाभ द्यावा; निकाल फक्त पक्षकारांपुरता (in personam) असेल तर इतरांनी विलंब नसल्याचे दाखवावे; "कुंपणावर बसलेल्यांना" लाभ नाही — हीच भूमिका DoPT/सा.प्र.वि. घेते आणि *सिद्दराज* स्पष्टीकरण हे त्याचे उदाहरण.
