# 40-C  Case-law: Regularisation of temporary / ad-hoc / daily-wage / contract employees; Kalelkar Award

**Scope**: Claims by daily-wage sweepers, peons, water-men, drivers, data-entry operators, Court Managers, outsourced staff attached to District/City Civil/Small Causes Courts for permanency, equal pay and regularisation; State's reliance on *Umadevi*; the post-2024 Supreme Court trend (*Vinod Kumar*, *Jaggo*, *Shripal*, *Dharam Singh*, *Pawan Kumar*) which petitioners now cite against the State; Kalelkar Award matters (PWD/Irrigation establishments — cited by analogy in court-staff cases).

## 2. Case details

### 2.1 Secretary, State of Karnataka v. Umadevi (3) (SC, 10.04.2006) ✖
- **Ratio** (standard summary): Public employment must follow Arts. 14 and 16 — open advertisement, equal opportunity, selection by rules. Persons engaged temporarily/on daily wages/contract outside this process acquire no right to be regularised merely by length of service; courts (Art. 226/32) must not direct regularisation/absorption/permanence because sympathy would defeat the constitutional scheme. Distinction between **illegal** appointments (no right at all) and **irregular** appointments (against sanctioned posts, by a competent authority, but with procedural irregularity). Interim orders of courts cannot be the basis of equity. **¶53**: Union/States to take a *one-time* step to regularise, as a one-time measure, services of irregularly appointed persons who have worked **10 years or more** in duly sanctioned posts, not under cover of court orders, within six months.
- **State uses**: (i) daily-wage/outsourced staff of courts were never appointed against sanctioned posts through the Recruitment Rules (District Courts Recruitment Rules / High Court Rules under Art. 229); (ii) outsourcing through agencies under GAD GRs 2014–2023 (library file 81) is a policy choice; (iii) no vacancy/no sanctioned post; (iv) ¶53 is a one-time measure already exhausted; (v) court orders of status quo cannot be the basis of regularisation.
- **Petitioners use**: ¶53 + *M.L. Kesari* (10 years counted up to date of consideration) + *Narendra Kumar Tiwari* (cut-off cannot defeat the object) + the 2024–26 line (below) to argue "irregular, not illegal".

### 2.2 Narendra Kumar Tiwari v. State of Jharkhand (SC, 17.07.2018) ✖
- Jharkhand framed Regularisation Rules 2015 requiring 10 years' service *as on 10.04.2006*. Held: the purpose of Umadevi ¶53 was to give a one-time benefit to long-serving irregular appointees; a cut-off that excludes those who completed 10 years later (but before the Rules) is arbitrary; Rules to be applied counting 10 years up to the date of the Rules.
- **Maharashtra relevance**: when the State/High Court frames any one-time scheme (e.g. for Court Managers, computer operators), petitioners argue the cut-off must count service up to the scheme date.

### 2.3 Vinod Kumar v. Union of India (SC, 30.01.2024) ✔
- Source: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2016/21049/21049_2016_8_28_49942_Judgement_30-Jan-2024.pdf
- Appellants appointed Accounts Clerks in 1991–92 under a scheme after selection; served 25+ years; promoted by DPC. Allahabad HC had refused regularisation on Umadevi. SC allowed the appeal.
- **Verbatim ¶6**: "The application of the judgment in Uma Devi (supra) by the High Court does not fit squarely with the facts at hand". **¶7**: appointments "cannot be said to have been made illegally if they had followed the procedures of regular appointments". Status reclassified from temporary to regular; respondents to complete regularisation within three months.
- **State's distinction**: Vinod Kumar turned on a *selection process resembling regular recruitment* plus DPC promotion — absent in pure daily-wage/outsourced engagements.

### 2.4 Jaggo v. Union of India (SC, 20.12.2024) ✔
- Source: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2024/7264/7264_2024_6_1503_58145_Judgement_20-Dec-2024.pdf
- Part-time/temporary staff (sweepers, gardener, etc.) at Central Water Commission office, engaged for a decade+, terminated 27.10.2018 after outsourcing; CAT and Delhi HC refused relief. SC quashed termination and directed regularisation (no back wages; period counts for continuity and retiral benefits) — ¶28–29.
- **Verbatim ¶20**: "the decision in Uma Devi (supra) does not intend to penalize employees who have rendered long years of service"; prolonged continuous service can transform ad-hoc/temporary work into a case "demanding fair regularization".
- **Verbatim ¶25**: temporary contracts "have increasingly become a mechanism to evade long-term obligations owed to employees"; institutions "increasingly resort to outsourcing roles performed by temporary employees".
- **Verbatim ¶26**: "the laudable intent of the judgment is being subverted when institutions rely on its dicta" to reject claims — "effectively weaponizing it against employees who have rendered indispensable services over decades."
- **Petitioners use**: this is now the lead case against the State in every daily-wage court-staff petition, together with *Shripal* (2025), *Dharam Singh* (2025) and *Pawan Kumar* (2026).
- **State's answer**: (i) Jaggo did not overrule Umadevi (two-judge bench; Constitution Bench binding); (ii) relief turned on *termination* after long service plus *outsourcing of the same work* — where posts are filled by regular recruitment through MPSC/District Court selection and the daily-wager is not displaced, Jaggo is distinguishable; (iii) regularisation needs sanctioned posts and financial concurrence (Art. 229(2)/FD); (iv) Bombay HC must still apply Art. 14/16 tests case by case.

### 2.5 Shripal v. Nagar Nigam Ghaziabad (2025) ◐; Dharam Singh v. State of U.P. (19.08.2025) ◐; Pawan Kumar v. UOI (2026) ◐
- *Shripal*: daily-wage horticulture workers — regularisation; Umadevi not a shield (citation 2025 INSC 144 per secondary sources — verify).
- *Dharam Singh*: C.A. 8558/2018 — State's refusal to sanction posts on financial grounds held arbitrary; retrospective regularisation with supernumerary posts.
- *Pawan Kumar*: 2026 INSC 156 — similarly situated daily-wagers in the same department cannot be discriminated; regularisation from 2006.
- **State's reading**: all three involve (a) decades of continuous service, (b) perennial work, (c) discrimination between similarly placed workers or displacement by outsourcing. Affidavits must show absence of these features (nature of engagement, breaks, agency contract, no sanctioned post, no similarly placed regularised employee).

### 2.6 Kalelkar Award — Secretary, PWD v. Tukaram Pandurang Saraf (SC, 19.09.2024) ✔
- Source: https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2016/4586/4586_2016_14_1502_55860_Judgement_19-Sep-2024.pdf (from Bom HC Nagpur W.P. 3425/2010, 15.11.2014; Industrial Court Yavatmal Complaint (ULP) 38/2006).
- Background: the **Kalelkar Award** (Industrial Tribunal, 1967–70) governs work-charged/temporary/CTE staff of PWD & Irrigation Departments of Maharashtra; GR/Circular 27.05.1996 implemented it for certain categories. 
- **Verbatim ¶20 (Demand 5)**: work-charged, regular temporary and permanent categories "shall be entitled for the Government Holidays approved by the Government." (Demand 6): "All the employees shall be paid overtime allowance as per the provisions of section 14" of the Minimum Wages Act 1948. **¶21**: "except the daily-wage employees, all other categories of employees are entitled to get such public holidays". **¶22**: the 1996 Circular "does not govern the employees who were shifted to the Converted Temporary Establishment". **¶25**: "The appeal is dismissed accordingly. No costs."
- **Relevance to court staff**: petitioners (daily-wage court staff) sometimes borrow Kalelkar/CTE principles by analogy; the State's answer is that the Award binds only the PWD/Irrigation industrial establishments and specific categories, not judicial-department staff governed by Art. 229 / District Court Recruitment Rules; daily-wagers are excluded even under the Award (¶21).
- Bombay HC Kalelkar line (◐): several DB/SB decisions (Nagpur/Aurangabad, 2023–2025) reject the State's "future vacancy" plea and grant CTE/permanency benefits; verify individual citations before citing.

## 3. Drafting checklist — State's reply in regularisation writs of court staff
1. Mode of engagement: who engaged, under what sanction, whether against a sanctioned post, whether through agency/outsourcing GR; copies of agreements.
2. Length and continuity of service; artificial breaks; whether work is perennial.
3. Whether regular recruitment to the post has taken place / is in process (Recruitment Rules, MPSC, District Judge selection) — if yes, say so and offer age relaxation if policy permits.
4. Whether similarly placed persons were regularised (Pawan Kumar / Art. 14 risk).
5. Financial concurrence / Art. 229(2) approval status for creating posts.
6. Distinguish Jaggo/Vinod Kumar on facts; cite Umadevi ¶53 as exhausted one-time measure and Constitution-Bench supremacy.

## 4. मराठी सारांश
- *उमादेवी* (घटनापीठ, 2006): नियमबाह्य/तात्पुरत्या नेमणुकांना केवळ सेवाकालावरून नियमितीकरणाचा हक्क नाही; ¶53 — मंजूर पदावर 10+ वर्षे "अनियमित" (illegal नव्हे) सेवा केलेल्यांसाठी एकवेळची उपाययोजना.
- *नरेंद्रकुमार तिवारी*: नियमितीकरण योजनेतील कट-ऑफ ¶53 चा उद्देश निष्फळ करू शकत नाही; 10 वर्षे योजना तारखेपर्यंत मोजावीत.
- *विनोद कुमार* (2024) व *जग्गो* (2024): दीर्घ सेवा, निवड प्रक्रिया, आउटसोर्सिंगने काढून टाकणे — अशा प्रकरणांत उमादेवीचा "शस्त्रासारखा" वापर चालणार नाही; नियमितीकरणाचे निर्देश. *श्रीपाल*, *धरम सिंग*, *पवन कुमार* (2025–26) याच दिशेने.
- शासनाचे उत्तर: उमादेवी घटनापीठ अजूनही बंधनकारक; प्रत्येक प्रकरणात नेमणुकीचा प्रकार, मंजूर पद, सतत सेवा, समान स्थितीतील इतरांशी भेदभाव, नियमित भरतीची स्थिती हे मुद्दे दाखवावेत.
- कालेलकर पुरस्कार (*तुकाराम सराफ*, 2024): फक्त बांधकाम/सिंचन विभागातील CTE/कार्यप्रभारित कर्मचाऱ्यांना लागू; रोजंदारी वगळले; न्यायालयीन कर्मचाऱ्यांना साधर्म्याने लागू होत नाही असा बचाव.
