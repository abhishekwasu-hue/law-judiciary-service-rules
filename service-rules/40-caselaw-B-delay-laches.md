# 40-B  Case-law: Delay, laches, acquiescence and stale claims in service matters

**Scope**: The State's first line of defence in most court-staff writs (pay parity, seniority, promotion, regularisation, increment arrears filed years after the cause). Collected here: the Supreme Court tests for (i) when delay defeats a service claim, (ii) when a claim is a "continuing wrong", (iii) effect of repeated representations, (iv) "fence-sitters" who wake up after others' success.

## 2. Case details

### 2.1 C. Jacob v. Director of Geology & Mining (SC, 2008) ✖
- **Ratio**: A government servant who makes a representation decades after the cause (here, a workman claiming reinstatement many years after termination) cannot, by the device of a representation and its rejection, get a fresh cause of action. The Court cautioned High Courts/Tribunals against directing authorities to "consider and dispose of" old representations, because the resulting rejection order is then challenged as a fresh order — this is how stale claims get revived.
- **Typical passage (paraphrase — open the judgment before quoting)**: when a direction to consider a representation is issued "as a matter of course", the authority should state in its order that the claim is stale and that the direction to consider does not revive it.
- **State uses**: whenever the petitioner's cause is years old and only a recent "rejection letter" is impugned — plead that the rejection is not a fresh cause; mention the dates of the original cause and of the first representation. Useful also when the court itself directs the Law & Judiciary Dept. to "decide the representation": decide on merits *and* on delay.
- **Petitioners use**: argue the rejection order is the "impugned order" and the petition is within time.

### 2.2 Chairman, U.P. Jal Nigam v. Jaswant Singh (SC, 2006) ✖
- Employees retired at 58 under a rule; after a later SC decision (Harwindra Kumar) fixed the age at 60, many who had retired without protest sought re-fixation. Held: those who accepted retirement and did not litigate in time cannot now claim; a "fence-sitter" cannot get the benefit of a judgment obtained by others, since delay and acquiescence disentitle. The Court declined relief even on the "similarly situated" argument.
- **State uses**: against batch petitions filed after a single employee wins (e.g. after a Mundinamani or Shetty Commission ruling) — distinguish those who litigated in time from those who did not; see also *Arvind Kumar Srivastava* (in rem vs in personam — file I).

### 2.3 State of Uttaranchal v. Shiv Charan Singh Bhandari (SC, 2013) ✖
- Claim: promotion of juniors in 1986–87 challenged in 2004; Tribunal/HC granted notional promotion. SC reversed: the claim was stale; repeated representations and "mercy petitions" do not stop time running; a petitioner who waits for 18 years cannot claim promotion with retrospective effect to the prejudice of others.
- **State uses**: seniority / promotion / ACP-DPC claims raised long after the seniority list or DPC; emphasise third-party rights of juniors already promoted (Tarsem Singh exception).

### 2.4 Union of India v. N. Murugesan (SC, 29.09.2021) ◐
- Reported (2022) 2 SCC 25. The Court (Sundresh J) discussed delay, laches and acquiescence: acquiescence is "a tacit or passive acceptance" with knowledge (para 25, per secondary summary); a person who, knowing the terms, continues to act on them cannot later insist on different terms; the doctrine of "approbate and reprobate" bars taking inconsistent positions. The claim (service-related, raised late) failed.
- **Caution**: open the official text (sci.gov.in — judgment 29.09.2021) before quoting paragraph numbers.
- **State uses**: where the petitioner accepted a pay fixation / option / seniority position / retirement without protest and raises it years later; also where the petitioner took a benefit under one scheme (e.g. ACP) and now attacks the same scheme.

### 2.5 Chairman, SBI v. M.J. James (SC, 16.11.2021) ◐
- 2021 SCC OnLine SC 1061. Verbatim as quoted by SCC Online blog: "Laches unlike limitation is flexible." … "both limitation and laches destroy the remedy but not the right." … "acquiescence implies active assent and is based upon the rule of estoppel in pais." … "Even indirect acquiescence implies almost active consent, which is not to be inferred by mere silence or inaction which is involved in laches." … "Acquiescence in this manner is quite distinct from delay."
- **State uses**: to explain the distinction in the affidavit — petitioner's conduct (accepting pay, signing options, not appealing) shows acquiescence, not merely delay.
- **Petitioners use**: "mere silence" is not acquiescence; the right survives even if the remedy is weakened.

### 2.6 Bichitrananda Behera v. State of Orissa (SC, 11.10.2023) ✔
- Source opened: Orissa HC mirror of C.A. 6664/2023. Appellant appointed PET on 14.05.1994 and continuously working; a rival (Respondent 5) raised his claim after 12+ years and succeeded before Tribunal/HC. SC set aside: **¶20** — "the Respondent No.5 should have been non-suited on the ground of delay and laches, which especially in service matters" carry particular weight, together with acquiescence; appellant's service protected from 14.05.1994; Art. 142 used for a lump-sum to the rival with the rider that it is not a precedent.
- **State uses**: against claims that would unsettle an incumbent's long-standing position; emphasise third-party prejudice.

### 2.7 State of U.P. v. Arvind Kumar Srivastava (SC, 2014) ✖
- Three-judge bench. Principles (paraphrase): (i) normally, when a judgment is in rem / declares a general rule, similarly situated persons should be given the benefit without forcing them to litigate; (ii) **but** those who approach after unreasonable delay, having sat on the fence, are not entitled, because granting relief would unsettle settled matters; (iii) the earlier judgment's scope (in personam vs in rem) is decisive.
- **Cross-ref**: file I (judgment in personam vs in rem; DoPT stand).

## 3. Drafting checklist — delay plea in the State's affidavit
1. State the exact dates: cause of action (order/seniority list/DPC/retirement), first representation, rejection, filing of OA/WP.
2. Say whether the petitioner accepted the position (drew pay, signed option, did not appeal) → acquiescence (M.J. James; Murugesan).
3. Identify third parties who would be affected (juniors promoted, incumbents) → Tarsem Singh exception; Bhandari; Bichitrananda.
4. If the claim is a continuing wrong (pay/pension) — concede maintainability but seek the 3-year cap (Tarsem Singh) and, for increment cases, the Siddaraj cut-off (file A).
5. If the petitioner relies on another employee's judgment — plead Jaswant Singh / Arvind Kumar Srivastava (fence-sitter) and that the earlier judgment was in personam.
6. Note the Bombay HC practice of directing "decision on representation" — the decision must record both merits and the stale nature of the claim (C. Jacob).

## 4. मराठी सारांश
- सेवाविषयक दाव्यांत विलंब (delay/laches) हा शासनाचा पहिला बचाव: जुन्या कारणासाठी नंतर केलेले निवेदन व त्याचा नकार हा "नवा कारणकृत्य" ठरत नाही (*सी. जेकब*, *एम.के. सरकार*).
- "कुंपणावर बसलेले" कर्मचारी — दुसऱ्याने जिंकल्यावर जागे होणारे — यांना त्या निकालाचा लाभ नाही (*जसवंत सिंग*, *अरविंद कुमार श्रीवास्तव*); ज्येष्ठता/पदोन्नतीचे जुने दावे तृतीय पक्षांच्या हक्कांमुळे शिळे (*भंडारी*, *बिचित्रानंद बेहेरा* ¶20).
- *मुरुगेसन* व *एम.जे. जेम्स*: विलंब, laches आणि acquiescence (माहिती असूनही स्वीकार) यातील फरक — स्वीकारलेली स्थिती नंतर नाकारता येत नाही.
- निवृत्तिवेतन/वेतनासारखा "सततचा अन्याय" असल्यास दावा चालतो, पण थकबाकी 3 वर्षांपुरती (*तरसेम सिंग*).
- शपथपत्रात तारखा (कारण, निवेदन, नकार, याचिका), स्वीकृतीचे पुरावे व बाधित तृतीय पक्ष स्पष्टपणे नमूद करावेत.
