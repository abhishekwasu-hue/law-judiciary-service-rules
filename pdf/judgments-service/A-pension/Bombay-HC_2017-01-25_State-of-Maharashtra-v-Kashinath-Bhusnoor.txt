     O R A L    J U D G M E N T  (Per Smt.Vasanti    A   Naik, J.)


                       By this writ petition, the petitioner challenges the order of

     the Maharashtra Administrative Tribunal, dated 14/07/2008 directing

     the petitioner-State of Maharashtra to pay interest at the rate of 10%

     per   annum   to   the   petitioner   for   the   delayed   payment   of   the




                                                                                     
     commutation value of pension. 





                                                             
                      The respondent was working with  the petitioners at the

     relevant time as an awal karkun when he retired on attaining the age of




                                                            
     superannuation   on   31/05/1996.   Two   departmental   enquiries   were

     initiated against the respondent, while he was in service and they were




                                              
     continued   against   him,   after   his   retirement.   The   respondent   was
                             
     exonerated in the enquiries by the order dated 07/07/2000.  According

     to   the   petitioners,   the   respondent   was   paid   the   commuted   value   of
                            
     pension in the year 2002. In the year 2006, the respondent filed an

     original application seeking interest on the delayed payment of pension,

     gratuity and the commuted value of pension. The tribunal dismissed the
      


     original application in regard to the claim of the respondent for interest
   



     on   delayed   payment   of   gratuity   and   pension   however,   the   tribunal

     directed   the  petitioner   to   pay   interest   to   the   respondent   at  10%  per





     annum on the delayed payment of commuted value of pension.   The

     petitioners have challenged the part of the order of the tribunal that

     directs  the  petitioners  to   pay  the  interest  at  10%  per annum  on  the





     delayed payment of commuted value of pension. 




                      Ms   Udeshi,   the   learned   Assistant   Government   Pleader

     appearing for the petitioners, submitted that a retired employee is not

     entitled to receive the commuted value of the pension, if a departmental

     enquiry   is   pending   against   him   on   the   date   of   his   retirement.   The

     learned   Assistant   Government   Pleader   relied   on   rule   4   of   the




                                                                                        
     Maharashtra Civil Services (Commutation of Pension) Rules, 1984 to




                                                                
     substantiate   her   submission.   It   is   submitted   that   though   the   tribunal

     rightly held that the gratuity and pension was not liable to be paid to a

     retired employee, if a departmental enquiry was pending against him,




                                                               
     the tribunal, without referring to the provisions of rule 4 of the Rules of

     1984,   erroneously   held   that   the   petitioners   ought   to   have   paid   the




                                                
     commuted   value   of   pension   to   the   respondent   at   the   time   of   his
                             
     retirement in the year 1996. It is stated that the tribunal failed to notice

     the important provisions of rule 4 of the Rules of 1984 before partly
                            
     allowing the original application filed by the respondent. 




                      None appears for the respondent, though served.   None
      


     had appeared on behalf of the respondent on 24/01/2017 also.  

   



                      On hearing the learned Assistant Government Pleader for





     the petitioners and on a perusal of the impugned order, it appears that

     the   tribunal   was   not   justified   in   directing   the   petitioners   to   pay   the

     interest at the rate of 10% per annum on the delayed payment of the





     commuted   value   of   pension.   As   rightly   submitted   on   behalf   of   the

     petitioners, rule 4 of the Maharashtra Civil Services (Commutation of

     Pension) Rules, 1984 was not considered by the tribunal before partly

     allowing the original application filed by the respondent.  Rule 4 of the

     Rules of 1984 provides that a government servant would not be entitled

     to   commute  a  fraction  of   his  provisional   pension  till   a  departmental

     enquiry or judicial proceedings instituted against him before the date of




                                                                                       
     his retirement are culminated. In view of rule 4 of the Rules of 1984,




                                                             
     the   respondent   was   not   entitled   to   receive   the   commuted   value   of

     pension on the date of his retirement, till the departmental enquiry was

     concluded.   The   departmental   enquiry   was   concluded   against   the




                                                            
     respondent   on   07/07/2000   and   after   commuting   the   fraction   of   his

     pension,   the   petitioners   paid   the   amount   due   and   payable   to   the




                                              
     respondent, in February, 2002.  Since the tribunal has failed to consider
                             
     the material provisions of rule 4 of the Rules of 1984 while granting the

     relief in favour of the respondent, the impugned order is liable to be set
                            
     aside. 




                      Hence,   for   the   reasons   aforesaid,   the   writ   petition   is
      


     allowed. The impugned order, so far as it directs the petitioners to pay
   



     the interest at 10% per annum on the commuted value of pension to the

     respondent is hereby quashed and set aside. Rule is made absolute in





     the aforesaid terms with no order as to costs.
