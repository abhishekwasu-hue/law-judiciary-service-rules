          3.                 Denial of family pension and other consequential benefits

          to the petitioner widow has occasioned to resort the jurisdiction of this

          Court in terms of Article 226 of the Constitution of India. The

          Petitioner's husband Dnyaneshwar Chafle was appointed as 'Leprosy

          Technician' by the order of Joint Director of Health Services dated

          15.1.1973 in the services of respondent No.1 Zilla Parishad,

          Chandrapur. Accordingly, Dnyaneshwar Chafle joined the services on

          25.1.1973 and continued for a long period. However, from 22.6.2002

          Dnyaneshwar Chafle remained absent since he went missing.                      His

          whereabouts were not known for a decade and eventually he died on

          28.7.2013. Petitioner being widow has enquired with the respondent

          about retiremental benefits accrued to her on account of 29 years

          services rendered by Dnyaneshwar Chafle and upon his death the

          petitioner learnt that deceased Dnyaneshwar Chafle was suspended on

          26.3.2003 and later on terminated from services vide order dated

          24.10.2003. The petitioner was unaware about said proceedings.

          Hence, she obtained said information by resorting the provisions of

          Right     to Information Act.      The petitioner made representations for

          release of family pension and consequential benefits. However, it was

          denied      vide     impugned    communications    dated     25.09.2014        and

          21.08.2018.

          4.               It is the petitioners case that deceased Dnyaneshwar

          Chafle had rendered the pensionable service i.e. of 29 years. Neither

          notice of suspension nor termination order was served on her husband.

          The bone of contention is about non holding of departmental enquiry

          against deceased Dnyaneshwar Chafle. According to petitioner, since

          no enquiry was held against Dnyaneshwar Chafle, the action of denial

          of pensionary benefits is per se illegal.





          5.               Per contra, it is the stand of respondent that the petitioner

          went missing from 22.6.2002 and his whereabouts were not known.

          The department has made every endeavour to serve suspension and

          termination order to the petitioner personally, but he was not available

          at known places. The department has also issued public notice dated

          27.9.2003 by which the petitioner was called upon to join the services.

          The respondent has produced the acknowledgement about show cause

          notice purportedly received by the father of Dnyaneshwar Chafle on

          14.10.2002. Ultimately, it is submitted that, though show cause notice

          was served, the petitioner remained absent and therefore, the

          termination order which attained finality is immuned from challenge.

          6.                 Needless to say that termination of an employee could

          have under Maharashtra Zilla Parishad District Services (Discipline and

          Appeal) Rules, 1964. In case of imposition of major penalty it can be

          only by holding regular enquiry.           The acknowledgement dated

          14.10.2002 would not serve any purpose since it preceeded to the show

          cause notice dated 26.3.2003.       Pertinent to note that the petitioner

          being unheard about termination action, has obtained information

          under Right to Information Act from the respondent. Interesting to note

          that concerned Public Information Officer vide letter dated 6.2.2014 has

          informed in unambiguous terms that deceased Dnyaneshwar Chafle

          was terminated without conducting any departmental enquiry. Rule

          6(1) of the Maharashtra Zilla Parishad District Services (Discipline and

          Appeal) Rules,1964 provides that, no order of imposing penalties

          specified in clause (iv) to (viii) of Rule 4 shall be imposed without

          enquiry. The penalty of dismissal from service is one of them which

          falls under clause -(vii) of Rule 4, hence the order of dismissal without

          enquiry is unsustainable in the eyes of law. Neither suspension order

          dated 26.03.2003 nor termination order dated 24.10.2003 speaks about

          holding of full-fledge departmental enquiry by following rules of natural

          justice. Therefore, the impugned action of termination/dismissal

          without holding any enquiry is per se illegal.

          7.               The petitioner has specifically stated that the order of

          dismissal was not served on deceased employee.                             Mere     paper

          publication or service on the family member is not enough. Reliance is

          placed     on     the      judgment     in   case     of   Union      of    India     and

          ors..Vrs..Dinanath Shantaram Karekar and ors reported in (1998)7

          SCC 569, wherein the Apex Court ruled that where the disciplinary

          proceedings are initiated or to be initiated by issuing chargesheet its

          actual service is essential, so as to accord an opportunity to the

          employee to submit reply . Though respondent has produced a photo

          copy of one undated acknowledgement purportedly signed by deceased

          employee, however, sans authenticity, the said piece of paper cannot be

          considered.       The order of dismissal would not breath, unless it is

          communicated          to      the   concerned.      Obviously,     there    is    deemed

          continuation which enable the heirs of Dnyaneshwar Chafle to get all

          pensionary benefits. The petitioner also relied on Government

          Resolution No.PEN/1089/491/SER-4 issued by Finance Department on

          5th July, 1991, wherein it was resolved to accord pensionary benefits to

          the family of government employees, who went missing. In order to

          buttress her case, the petitioner relied upon reported case of Pramila

          Pandit Sagat..Vrs..State of Maharashtra and others, 2007(3)Mh.L.J,

          478, wherein this Court on similar grounds by giving benefit of
