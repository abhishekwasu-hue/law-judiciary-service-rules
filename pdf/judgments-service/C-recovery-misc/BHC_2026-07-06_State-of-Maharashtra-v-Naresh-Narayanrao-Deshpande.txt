            1.       The respondent was working as Executive Engineer in the Integrated

            Unit Public Works Division, Nagpur. On observing some irregularities in

            the allotment of work to the unemployed engineers by making

            false/forged documents during the period 2006-2007, inquiry was

            conducted against the respondent. The respondent during the pendency

            of the said inquiry got superannuated. The inquiry was continued even

            thereafter and as such concluded after the retirement of the respondent.

            2.       Since the respondent got retired before the inquiry was concluded,

            he was punished with the deduction of 10% amount from the pension

            payable to him. The said punishment is held to be bad and liable to be set

            aside by the Maharashtra Administrative Tribunal in an Original

            Application filed by the respondent.

            3.       The State has approached this Court by filing the present petition

            raising a challenge to the judgment and order passed by the Maharashtra

                             2                           27-WP-4432-2023.odt

Administrative Tribunal, Nagpur Bench, Nagpur, dated 13 th December,

2022, thereby allowing Original Application No.133 of 2019 filed by the

respondent and setting aside the order of punishment of deduction of 10%

amount from the pension payable to the respondent.

4.   While pointing out the perversity in the impugned judgment and

order, Ms T.H. Khan, learned Assistant Government Pleader for the

petitioners, has brought to the notice of this Court certain incorrect

statements recorded by the learned Tribunal, which, according to her, are

contrary to record.

5.   In reply to the same, Shri A.S. Deshpande, learned counsel for the

respondent, has argued that the learned Tribunal has failed to consider the

relevant material, and if such material is taken into consideration, the fate

of the matter would not change even after taking into consideration such

findings recorded by the Tribunal, which, according to the learned

Assistant Government Pleader, are contrary to record.

6.   Shri Deshpande, learned counsel, has further argued that the order

of the learned Tribunal needs to be maintained on the ground that there

was no prior notice issued to the respondent of informing continuation of

the departmental inquiry against the respondent after his superannuation.

7.   It is submitted that such notice under Rule 27(2)(a) of the

Maharashtra Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1982 ('Pension Rules') is
                            3                          27-WP-4432-2023.odt

required for the reason that when the inquiry was initiated against the

respondent, it was initiated under the Maharashtra Civil Services

(Discipline and Appeal) Rules, 1979 ('Discipline and Appeal Rules') and

therefore, had any punishment imposed prior to the retirement of the

respondent, that would have been under the Discipline and Appeal Rules.

However, because of superannuation of the respondent during the

pendency of the departmental inquiry, since no punishment could be

imposed under the Discipline and Appeal Rules, the only punishment

which could be imposed was under the Pension Rules and therefore, in the

changed circumstances, such notice to the respondent was necessary about

continuation of the departmental inquiry, informing thereby that at the

conclusion of the departmental inquiry if the respondent is found guilty,

the punishment would be under the Pension Rules.

8.   The learned counsel submits that as there was no such notice issued

in this case, the learned Tribunal has held in favour of the respondent. He

therefore submits that even if the statement of the learned Assistant

Government Pleader is accepted that there is perversity in recording the

findings on merits, the respondent is still having a good case, for the

above-referred reason that there was no notice given to the petitioner

under Section 27 of the Pension Rules.

9.   Shri Deshpande, learned counsel for the respondent, has drawn

attention of this Court to the judgment of the learned Single Judge of this
                            4                          27-WP-4432-2023.odt

Court in the case of Chairman/Secretary of Institute of Shri Acharya Ratna

Deshbhushan Shikshan Prasarak Mandal, Kolhapur and another Vs.

Bhujgonda B. Patil, reported in 2003(3) Mh.L.J. 602, wherein the learned

Single Judge has observed thus :

     "12. Rule 27(1) of the Pension Rules provides that :-

   "Government may, by order in writing, withhold or withdraw a pension
   or any part of it, whether permanently or for a specified period, and
   also order the recovery from such pension, the whole or part of any
   pecuniary loss caused to Government, if, in any departmental or
   judicial proceedings, the pensioner is found guilty of grave misconduct
   or negligence during the period of his service including service
   rendered upon re-employment after retirement;

   Provided that the Maharashtra Public Service Commission shall be
   consulted before any final orders are passed in respect of officers
   holding posts within their purview:

   Provided further that where a part of pension is withheld or
   withdrawn, the amount of remaining pension shall not be reduced
   below the minimum fixed by Government."

Apparently, the provision of law contended in Sub-rule (1) of Rule 27 of
the Pension Rules, therefore, empowers the Government to pass an order
withholding or withdrawing a pension if in any departmental or judicial
proceedings the pensioner is found to be guilty of grave misconduct or
negligence either during the period of his service or during the period of
his re-employment. Apparently, Rule 27(1) is comprised of two parts. The
first part speaks of power of the Government to pass an order regarding
reduction or withdrawal of pension. The second part deals with the
circumstances in which such an order can be passed. The Rule nowhere
empowers the Government to initiate or continue the disciplinary
proceedings after the employee attains the age of superannuation. The
Rule is meant for and confined to the power of Government to reduce or
withdraw the pension of a pensioner on account of proved grave
misconduct or negligence of such pensioner while he was in service.
Besides, the Rule 2(a) of Rule 27 clarifies that the proceedings spoken of
for the purpose of order relating to pension under Rule 27(1) though
initially may be for disciplinary action while the pensioner was in service,
those proceedings would be deemed to have been continued only for the
                            5                          27-WP-4432-2023.odt

purpose of action under Rule 27(1) relating to the pension and not for
disciplinary action. Sub-rule (2)(a) of Rule 27 of the pension Rules reads
thus:-

   "The departmental proceedings referred to in sub-rule (1), if instituted
   while the Government servant was in service whether before his
   retirement or during his re-employment, shall, after the final
   retirement of the Government servant, be deemed to be proceedings
   under this rule and shall be continued and concluded by the authority
   by which they were commenced in the same manner if the
   Government servant had continued in service."

The above clause, therefore, in clear terms provides that the departmental
proceedings initiated for disciplinary action can be continued after the
employee attains the age of superannuation only for the purposes of
reduction or withdrawal of the pension and gratuity and not for the
purpose of disciplinary action. Further, Clause (a) of sub-rule (6) thereof
provides that "for the purpose of the said rule, departmental proceedings
shall be deemed to be instituted on the date on which the statement of
charges is issued to be Government servant or pensioner, or if the
Government servant has been placed under suspension from an earlier
date, on such date."

"14. The fact that the proceedings are continued only to deal with the
issue of reduction or withdrawal of pension is necessarily required to be
made known to the employee even though there is no specific provision in
that regard in Rule 27. The observations by the Apex Court in Brahm Datt
Sharma's case are to the effect that the opportunity of hearing in that
regard to the employee is necessary as any order of reduction or
withdrawal of pension could affect the right of the employee to receive
full pension. Principles of natural justice, therefore, need to be complied
with in all the proceedings under Rule 27, to the extent that an
opportunity of being heard must be offered to the employee before an
order under Rule 27(1) is passed."

"15. In the case in hand, as already seen above, it is not in dispute that
the respondent was suspended on 24.11.1992. It is also not in dispute that
charge sheet for preliminary inquiry held against the respondent was
served upon the respondent on 28.12.1996. It is also not in dispute that
the suspension was with the intention to initiate disciplinary proceedings
on the ground of misconduct on the part of respondent. It is also
undisputed fact that the charge sheet served upon the respondent on
28.12.1996 was in relation to the disciplinary proceedings. Besides the
same was served after the respondent had attained the age of
superannuation. In the background of undisputed facts if one reads the

                             6                           27-WP-4432-2023.odt

provisions contained in Sub-rule (6)(a) of Rule 27 and bearing in mind
the expression "departmental proceedings" relates to the continuation of
such proceedings only for the purpose of decision pertaining to the
payment of pension, neither any enquiry was held under Rule 27(1) nor
the disciplinary proceedings allegedly commenced during the service
tenure of the respondent were continued for the purpose of order relating
to pension alone. It is pertinent to note that the petitioners have not
brought to the notice of this court any other provision either in the
Disciplinary Rules or otherwise empowering the petitioners to commence
disciplinary proceedings or to continue the same after the employee
attains age of superannuation and, the reliance in that regard was placed
only on the provisions contained in Rule 27 of the Pension Rules. For the
reasons stated above the provisions contained in Rule 27 cannot be of help
to the petitioners either to justify initiation of disciplinary proceedings or
continuation thereof after the respondent had attained the age of
superannuation. The Apex Court in Bhagarathi Jena's case (supra) while
dealing with the issue as to whether continuation of disciplinary inquiry
for the purpose of decision regarding reduction of retiral benefits payable
to the employee clearly ruled that in the absence of provisions for
conducting disciplinary inquiry after retirement of employee, the
corporation therein had no legal authority to make any reduction in the
retiral benefits of the employee on the basis of findings in such inquiry.
The decision apparently rules that for the purpose of initiation or
continuation of disciplinary proceedings or inquiry in that regard on
attainment of age of superannuation by employee, there must be specific
provision of law in that regard. In the absence of any such authority, the
inquiry, even if is initiated during the tenure of service or before the
employee attains the age of superannuation, the same is to be deemed to
have been lapsed on the employee attaining the age of superannuation.
The relevant observation of the Apex Court in the decision of Bhagarathi
Jena's case which reveals this ruling reads thus:-

   "In view of the absence of such provision in the above said regulations,
   it must be held that the Corporation had no legal authority to make
   any reduction in the retiral benefits of the appellant. There is also no
   provision for conducting a disciplinary enquiry after retirement of the
   appellant nor any provision stating that in case misconduct is
   established, a deduction could be made from retiral benefits. Once the
   appellant had retired from service on 30.6.1995, there was no
   authority vested in the Corporation for continuing the departmental
   enquiry even for the purpose of imposing any reduction in the retiral
   benefits payable to the appellant. In the absence of such authority, it
   must be held that the enquiry had lapsed and the appellant was
   entitled to full retiral benefits on retirement."

                            7                         27-WP-4432-2023.odt

Undoubtedly, in case where the provision of law permits imposition of
reduction on the payment of pension by holding necessary inquiry in that
regard or continuing the departmental inquiry only for that purpose,
certainly the employer can order reduction or withdrawal of the pension,
as is the case under Rule 27 of the Pension Rules in force in the State of
Maharashtra. However, as already observed above those proceedings after
attaining the age of superannuation can be only for the purpose of
deciding the issue of pension and cannot have any link with the
disciplinary proceedings. Once the disciplinary proceedings, in the
absence of authority to continue the same after the retirement of the
employee, are to be held as lapsed, such proceedings cannot enure to the
benefit of the employer to impose penalty under Rule 27 in relation to the
pension without adhering to the procedure prescribed under the said
Rule 27 itself."

10.   Similarly, the Co-ordinate Bench of this Court in the case of

Manohar B. Patil Vs. State of Maharashtra and others, reported in 2013(6)

Mh.L.J. 311, has held in Para 15 thus :

      "15. Sub-Rule (1) of Rule 27 of the Pension Rules deals with
      powers of the Government of reducing or withholding pension. It
      also confers power to recover from pension the amount of financial
      loss caused to the Government due to misconduct on the part of a
      retired employee. The power to withdraw or to reduce pension or
      power to recover pecuniary loss caused to the Government can be
      exercised provided the Pensioner is found guilty of misconduct or
      negligence. Thus, Rule 27 permits initiation of departmental
      proceedings against the Pensioner for a limited purposes of taking
      action as provided in Sub-Rule (1) of Rule 27 of the Pension Rules.
      We have already adverted to Clause (b) of Sub-Rule (2) of Rule 27
      which provides that the departmental proceedings cannot be
      instituted after superannuation save with the sanction of the
      Government and the proceedings shall not be in respect of any
      event which took place more than four years before such institution.
      If the interpretation that a departmental inquiry cannot be initiated
      against the Pensioner even for limited purposes of taking action
      under Sub-Rule (1) of Rule 27 of the Pension Rules is accepted,
      Clause (b) of sub-Rule (1) of Rule 27 of the Pension Rules becomes
      completely redundant. Sub-clauses (i) and (ii) of Clause (b) of
      Sub-Rule (2) of Rule 27 of the Pension Rules provide for safeguards
                            8                         27-WP-4432-2023.odt

      to ensure that a Pensioner is not unduly harassed by initiation of
      departmental proceedings. Thus we hold that Rule 27 permits
      institution of departmental proceedings after superannuation of an
      employee only for the purposes of taking action contemplated by
      Sub-Rule (1) of Rule 27 in relation to pension and in the said
      proceedings, no penalty can be imposed in accordance with the
      Discipline and Appeal Rules. To that extent, we approve the view
      taken by the learned Single Judge in the case of Shri Acharya Ratna
      Deshbhushan Shikshan Prasarak Mandal, 2003(3) Mh.L.J. 602 =
      2003(5) Bom.C.R. 197."

11.   It is evident from the judgment in the case of Bhujgonda B. Patil

(supra) that though the learned Single Judge has observed that there is no

provision to issue notice of continuation of inquiry proceedings, despite

the same, the learned Single Judge has observed that such notice of

continuation of inquiry is necessary. Such conclusion was arrived at by the

learned Single Judge by placing reliance on the judgment in the case of

State of Uttar Pradesh Vs. Brahm Datt Sharma and another, reported in

(1987) 2 SCC 179. After going through the aforesaid judgment, it is

evident that the Hon'ble Supreme Court had an occasion in the said case

to consider the situation where there was a Regulation issued under the

Punjab Civil Pension Rules, which says that if the service of a government

servant has not been thoroughly satisfactory, the authority competent to

sanction the pension is empowered to make such reduction in the amount

of pension as it may think proper.     The regulation does not expressly

provide for affording opportunity to the government servant before order

for the reduction in the pension, is issued. The Hon'ble Supreme Court
                            9                         27-WP-4432-2023.odt

has, therefore, observed that the principles of natural justice ordain that

opportunity of hearing must be afforded to the government servant before

any order is passed.

12.   It is evident that in the said matter, the Hon'ble Supreme Court was

not considering any rule similar to Rule 27 of the Pension Rules, which

reads thus :

      27. Right of Government to withhold or withdraw pension.

      (1) Appointing Authority may, by order in writing, withhold or
      withdraw a pension or any part of it whether permanently or for a
      specified period, and also order the recovery, from such pension, the
      whole or part of any pecuniary loss caused to Government, if, in any
      departmental or judicial proceedings, the pensioner is found guilty
      of grave misconduct or negligence during the period of his service
      including service rendered upon re-employment after retirement:

      Provided that the Maharashtra Public Service Commission shall be
      consulted before any final orders are passed in respect of officers
      holding posts within their purview:

      Provided further that where a part of pension is withheld or
      withdrawn, the amount of remaining pension shall not be reduced
      below the minimum fixed by Government.

      2) (a) The departmental proceedings referred to in sub-rule (1), if
      Instituted while the Government servant was in service whether
      before his retirement or during his re-employment, shall, after the
      final retirement of the Government Servant, be deemed to be
      proceedings under this rule and shall be continued and concluded
      by the authority by which they were commenced in the same
      manner as if the Government servant had continued in service.

      (b) ...

      (3) ...

      (4) ...

      (5) ...

      (6) ...

13.   From the language of Rule 27 of the Pension Rules, more

particularly Rule 27(2)(a), which is applicable to this case, it is evident

that there is no provision to issue notice by the employer to the employee

informing continuation of inquiry proceedings. On the contrary, if the

inquiry was initiated before the retirement of the employee and it

continued even after the superannuation of the employee, the above-

referred Rule says that the departmental proceedings be deemed to be the

proceedings under the Pension Rules and shall be continued and

concluded in the same manner as if the government servant has continued

in service.

14.   The Legislature in its wisdom appears to have not provided a

provision to issue a notice for continuation of departmental inquiry, which

was initiated during the service of an employee and continued even after

his/her retirement. The reason would be that under the Discipline and

Appeal Rules, the punishment which could be imposed are of such nature

which cannot be imposed once the employee has got retired. For instance,

warning, withholding of increment, reduction in rank, termination or

dismissal, etc.

15.   The reason for not providing such notice by the Legislature must be

that it is improbable that an employee who served for many years and is at
                                 11                            27-WP-4432-2023.odt

the fag end of the service and who is facing the inquiry cannot be said to

be not aware of the fact that if the inquiry continues after the retirement,

no punishment under the Discipline and Appeal Rules could be imposed,

though it was initiated under the Discipline and Appeal Rules. At the

same time, it is difficult to say that he/she does not possess knowledge

after serving for so many years in the Government about the provisions of

the Pension Rules, more particularly Rule 27, which permits punishment

under     the   said   Rules,    if   the     inquiry   continues   after   his/her

superannuation.

16.     The purpose and object in providing the Discipline and Appeal Rules

always to outline acceptable and unacceptable conduct of the employees

during performance of their duties, promote fairness and uniformity and

to ensure operational efficiency.           It is also with an object to correct

unwarranted behaviour and in certain cases, if necessary, to punish with

due process of law.

17.     In this backdrop, if any employee is found as guilty at the end of the

departmental inquiry on appreciation of available evidence on record,

such employee is set free on the ground that no notice of continuation of

inquiry was issued, the whole purpose of Section 27 of the Pension Rules

would defeat.

                            12                          27-WP-4432-2023.odt

18.   The purpose of Rule 27 is very clear from its language that, if in any

departmental or judicial proceeding the pensioner is found guilty of grave

misconduct during the period of his/her service including the service

rendered upon re-employment after retirement, the government may be

able to withhold or withdraw a pension or any part of it whether

permanently or for a specified period, and also order the recovery, from

such pension, the whole or part of any pecuniary loss caused to

government.

19.   The provisions of Rule 27 of the Pension Rules does not specify any

condition requiring the issuance of a notice for continuation of inquiry

after superannuation of an employee, under the said Rule. Thus, reading

such a condition into Rule 27 of the Pension Rules would amount to

adding in it something which is not provided by the Legislature

intentionally.

20.   Thus, according to us, the notice for continuation of departmental

proceedings after the superannuation of an employee is not required, as

the same is not provided under Rule 27 of the Pension Rules. However, in

view of the principles of natural justice and the well-settled principles of

service jurisprudence, the show cause notice before imposing any

punishment, as provided under Rule 27 of the Pension Rules, in the case of
                             13                         27-WP-4432-2023.odt

continuation of departmental inquiry even after superannuation of an

employee, would be mandatory.

21.      In the circumstances, we do not find the law laid down by the

learned Single Judge in the case of Bhujgonda B. Patil (supra), holding

that a notice of continuation of departmental proceedings under

Rule 27(2)(a) of the Pension Rules is necessary where the inquiry was

initiated    before   superannuation   and    it   continued   even    after

superannuation of an employee, is not a correct law and therefore the

judgment in the case of Bhujgonda B. Patil (supra) is held to be

per incuriam to the extent it holds that a notice of continuation of inquiry

is necessary under Rule 27 and if no such notice is issued, the inquiry

vitiates.

22.      As far as the judgment of the Co-ordinate Bench of this Court in

Manohar B. Patil (supra) is concerned, the judgment in the case of

Bhujgonda B. Patil (supra) was approved by the Co-ordinate Bench to the

extent the learned Single Judge has held that Rule 27 of the Pension Rules

permits institution of departmental proceedings after superannuation of

an employee only for the purpose of taking action contemplated by sub-

rule (1) of Rule 27 in relation to pension and in such proceedings no

penalty can be imposed in accordance with the Discipline and Appeal

Rules.

                             14                         27-WP-4432-2023.odt

23.   Having held so, if the findings recorded by the learned Tribunal are

considered, it is evident that one of the grounds to hold in favour of the

respondent was that no prior notice under Rule 27 was given by the State

to the respondent.

24.   In view of the above-referred observations, we have no hesitation to

hold that such findings recorded by the Tribunal are bad in law and the

same are liable to be quashed and set aside.

25.   Furthermore, after going through the inquiry report, it is evident

that there is a categorical finding recorded by the Inquiry Officer that all

the three charges against the respondent were proved. Despite the same,

the Tribunal has wrongly held that the Inquiry Officer has not stated

anything against the respondent.

26.   Thus, it is apparent that the findings recorded by the Tribunal are

perverse and not supported by documentary evidence and therefore, such

findings are contrary to record.

27.   In the circumstances, we are of the opinion that the matter needs to

be remanded back to the learned Tribunal for passing the order afresh

after reconsidering the matter on merits and without holding that the

inquiry vitiates for non-issuance of notice under Rule 27 of the Pension

Rules for continuation of the departmental proceedings.

28.   However, it is made clear that the point, as argued by the learned

counsel for the respondent, that no prior show cause notice was given
                                15                        27-WP-4432-2023.odt

  before imposing the penalty in question, i.e. reduction of 10% amount

  from the pension payable to the respondent, is kept open to be decided by

  the learned Tribunal while reconsidering the matter on merits.

  29.      Considering the fact that the respondent has already superannuated

  long back, the Tribunal may decide the matter expeditiously and in any

  case within a period of three months from the date of this order.

  30.      Since we have made the matter time-bound, we direct the parties to

  appear before the learned Tribunal on 15 th July, 2026 by placing the copy

  of this order on the record of the Tribunal.

  31.      With the above observations and directions, the petition stand

  disposed of.
