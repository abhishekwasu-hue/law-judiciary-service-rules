## A. Recovery of excess payment (Rafiq Masih line, undertakings, DCRG)

### 1. State of Punjab and others v. Rafiq Masih (White Washer) and others

- **File:** `SC_2014-12-18_State-of-Punjab-v-Rafiq-Masih.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal No. 11527 of 2014 (arising out of SLP(C) No. 11684 of 2012) with connected appeals; 18.12.2014
- **Bench:** Jagdish Singh Khehar and Arun Mishra, JJ.
- **Citation:** (2015) 4 SCC 334; AIR 2015 SC 696
- **Facts:** Employees of the State of Punjab were paid emoluments in excess of entitlement by mistake of the employer; recoveries ordered by the State were set aside by the Punjab and Haryana High Court. The State appealed.
- **Holding:** Court listed five situations in which recovery of excess payment from employees is impermissible in law (Class III/IV employees; retired or due to retire within one year; excess payment for more than five years before the order of recovery; higher post wrongly assigned; any other case where recovery would be iniquitous, harsh or arbitrary). Counsel for the State informed the Court that all the cases fell within the first four categories; the High Court orders quashing the recovery were deemed upheld and the appeals disposed of.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 12) “12. It is not possible to postulate all situations of hardship, which would govern employees on the issue of recovery, where payments have mistakenly been made by the employer, in excess of their entitlement. Be that as it may, based on the decisions referred to herein above, we may, as a ready reference, summarise the following few situations, wherein recoveries by the employers, would be impermissible in law: (i) Recovery from employees belonging to Class-III and Class-IV service (or Group 'C' and Group 'D' service). (ii) Recovery from retired employees, or employees who are due to retire within one year, of the order of recovery. (iii) Recovery from employees, when the excess payment has been made for a period in excess of five years, before the order of recovery is issued. (iv) Recovery in cases where an employee has wrongfully been required to discharge duties of a higher post, and has been paid accordingly, even though he should have rightfully been required to work against an inferior post. (v) In any other case, where the Court arrives at the conclusion, that recovery if made from the employee, would be iniquitous or harsh or arbitrary to such an extent, as would far outweigh the equitable balance of the employer's right to recover.”
  - (para 13) “13. We are informed by the learned counsel representing the appellant- State of Punjab, that all the cases in this bunch of appeals, would undisputedly fall within the first four categories delineated hereinabove. In the appeals referred to above, therefore, the impugned orders passed by the High Court of Punjab and Haryana (quashing the order of recovery), shall be deemed to have been upheld, for the reasons recorded above.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Primary authority on the limits of recovery of excess payment from Government servants (pay fixation errors); applied in Bombay High Court recovery cases (Nilam Naik, Darunte).

### 2. High Court of Punjab and Haryana v. Jagdev Singh

- **File:** `SC_2016-07-29_High-Court-of-PH-v-Jagdev-Singh.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal No. 3500 of 2006; 29.07.2016
- **Bench:** T.S. Thakur, CJI and Dr. D.Y. Chandrachud, J.
- **Citation:** (2016) 14 SCC 267; AIR 2016 SC 3523
- **Facts:** A judicial officer of Haryana furnished an undertaking to refund any excess payment when he opted for a revised pay scale (2001 Pay Rules). After the 2003 revised pay rules, a recovery of Rs. 1,22,003/- was ordered (letter dated 18.02.2004) after his compulsory retirement; the High Court set aside the recovery.
- **Holding:** Proposition (ii) of Rafiq Masih (no recovery from retired employees) does not apply where the officer furnished an undertaking at the time of opting for the revised scale; the officer is bound by the undertaking. Recovery to be made in equated monthly instalments over two years.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 9) “9 The submission of the Respondent, which found favour with the High Court, was that a payment which has been made in excess cannot be recovered from an employee who has retired from the service of the state. This, in our view, will have no application to a situation such as the present where an undertaking was specifically furnished by the officer at the time when his pay was initially revised accepting that any payment found to have been made in excess would be liable to be adjusted. While opting for the benefit of the revised pay scale, the Respondent was clearly on notice of the fact that a future re-fixation or revision may warrant an adjustment of the excess payment, if any, made.”
  - (para 11) “11 The principle enunciated in proposition (ii) above cannot apply to a situation such as in the present case. In the present case, the officer to whom the payment was made in the first instance was clearly placed on notice that any payment found to have been made in excess would be required to be refunded. The officer furnished an undertaking while opting for the revised pay scale. He is bound by the undertaking.”
  - (para 12) “12 For these reasons, the judgment of the High Court which set aside the action for recovery is unsustainable. However, we are of the view that the recovery should be made in reasonable instalments. We direct that the recovery be made in equated monthly instalments spread over a period of two years.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Undertaking-based recovery from a retired judicial officer; Rafiq Masih proposition (ii) not available where an undertaking was furnished.

### 3. Thomas Daniel v. State of Kerala and others

- **File:** `SC_2022-05-02_Thomas-Daniel-v-State-of-Kerala.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal No. 7115 of 2010; 02.05.2022
- **Bench:** S. Abdul Nazeer and Vikram Nath, JJ.
- **Citation:** Citation not shown in the source copy
- **Facts:** Increments granted to a school teacher/Headmaster (promoted 1989) were sought to be recovered by notice dated 09.10.1997 on an audit objection; he retired on 31.03.1999 and the recovery was pressed about ten years after retirement. Excess payment was due to a mistake in interpreting Kerala Service Rules; no fraud or misrepresentation was alleged.
- **Holding:** Recovery of the increments after ten years of retirement held unjustified; the High Court judgments, the order of 26.06.2000 and the recovery notice of 09.10.1997 were set aside.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 14) “(14) Coming to the facts of the present case, it is not contended before us that on account of the misrepresentation or fraud played by the appellant, the excess amounts have been paid. The appellant has retired on 31.03.1999. In fact, the case of the respondents is that excess payment was made due to a mistake in interpreting Kerala Service Rules which was subsequently pointed out by the Accountant General.”
  - (para 15) “(15) Having regard to the above, we are of the view that an attempt to recover the said increments after passage of ten years of his retirement is unjustified.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Recovery from a retiree after long lapse of time where no misrepresentation by the employee; reproduces Syed Abdul Qadir and Rafiq Masih.

### 4. Syed Abdul Qadir and others v. State of Bihar and others

- **File:** `SC_2008-12-16_Syed-Abdul-Qadir-v-State-of-Bihar.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal Nos. 3351-3354 of 2003 with connected appeals; 16.12.2008
- **Bench:** G.S. Singhvi, Harjit Singh Bedi and B.N. Agrawal, JJ.
- **Citation:** (2009) 3 SCC 475
- **Facts:** Excess amount was paid to teachers of Bihar secondary schools through a wrong interpretation of the Bihar Nationalised Secondary School (Service Conditions) Rules, 1983 (additional increment on promotion); the Finance Department admitted bona fide mistake; recovery was directed by the High Court Division Bench.
- **Holding:** No recovery of the excess amount ordered, since no misrepresentation or fraud by the teachers and many had retired or were about to retire; amounts already recovered to be refunded.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 28 (text as available; reproduced as para 59 in Thomas Daniel, 2022)) “28. Undoubtedly, the excess amount that has been paid to the appellants - teachers was not because of any misrepresentation or fraud on their part and the appellants also had no knowledge that the amount that was being paid to them was more than what they were entitled to. It would not be out of place to mention here that the Finance Department had, in its counter affidavit, admitted that it was a bona fide mistake on their part. The excess payment made was the result of wrong interpretation of the rule that was applicable to them, for which the appellants cannot be held responsible. Rather, the whole confusion was because of inaction, negligence and carelessness of the officials concerned of the Government of Bihar. Learned counsel appearing on behalf of the appellants-teachers submitted that majority of the beneficiaries have either retired or are on the verge of it. Keeping in view the peculiar facts and circumstances of the case at hand and to avoid any hardship to the appellants-teachers, we are of the view that no recovery of the amount that has been paid in excess to the appellants-teachers should be made.”
  - (para 29) “29. Learned counsel also submitted that prior to the interim order passed by this Court on 7.4.2003 in the special leave petitions, whereby the order of recovery passed by the Division Bench of the High Court was stayed, some instalments/amount had already been recovered from some of the teachers. Since we have directed that no recovery of the excess amount be made from the appellant- teachers and in order to maintain parity, it would be in the fitness of things that the amount that has been recovered from the teachers should be refunded to them.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** No-recovery direction where excess payment due to employer's wrong interpretation and employees retired; distinguished by Chandi Prasad Uniyal.

### 5. Chandi Prasad Uniyal and others v. State of Uttarakhand and others

- **File:** `SC_2012-08-17_Chandi-Prasad-Uniyal-v-State-of-Uttarakhand.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal No. 5899 of 2012 (arising out of SLP); 17.08.2012
- **Bench:** Dipak Misra and K.S. Radhakrishnan, JJ.
- **Citation:** (2012) 8 SCC 417; AIR 2012 SC 2951
- **Facts:** Over-payment to teachers/principals due to wrong fixation of 5th and 6th pay scales was sought to be recovered; the High Court Division Bench upheld recovery. Counsel for appellants relied on Shyam Babu Verma, Sahib Ram, Syed Abdul Qadir.
- **Holding:** Court held that the concept of fraud or misrepresentation is not a precondition for recovery; except for the few instances in Syed Abdul Qadir and Col. B.J. Akkara, excess payment due to wrong pay fixation can always be recovered. Recovery ordered in twelve equal monthly instalments.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 16) “16. We are concerned with the excess payment of public money which is often described as “tax payers money” which belongs neither to the officers who have effected over-payment nor that of the recipients. We fail to see why the concept of fraud or misrepresentation is being brought in such situations. Question to be asked is whether excess money has been paid or not may be due to a bona fide mistake. Possibly, effecting excess payment of public money by Government officers, may be due to various reasons like negligence, carelessness, collusion, favouritism etc. because money in such situation does not belong to the payer or the payee. Situations may also arise where both the payer and the payee are at fault, then the mistake is mutual. Payments are being effected in many situations without any authority of law and payments have been received by the recipients also without any authority of law. Any amount paid/received without authority of law can always be recovered barring few exceptions of extreme hardships but not as a matter of right, in such situations law implies an obligation on the payee to repay the money, otherwise it would amount to unjust enrichment.”
  - (para 17) “17. We are, therefore, of the considered view that except few instances pointed out in Syed Abdul Qadir case (supra) and in Col. B.J. Akkara (retd.) case (supra), the excess payment made due to wrong/irregular pay fixation can always be recovered.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Counter-line to Syed Abdul Qadir: recoverability of excess pay-fixation payments in the absence of fraud; to be read with Rafiq Masih and Thomas Daniel.

### 6. Shyam Babu Verma and others v. Union of India and others

- **File:** `SC_1994-02-08_Shyam-Babu-Verma-v-Union-of-India.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Petitions under Article 32 of the Constitution (numbers not shown in the source copy); 08.02.1994
- **Bench:** N.P. Singh, Jagdish Saran Verma and N. Venkatachala, JJ.
- **Citation:** (1994) 2 SCC 521
- **Facts:** Pharmacists in Central Hospital (Northern Railway) were given the pay scale of Rs. 330-560 from 1973; in 1984 the scale was reduced with effect from 01.01.1973 and recovery of the excess ordered (order dated 15.06.1984).
- **Holding:** Court held the petitioners entitled only to the scale of Rs. 330-480 on the Third Pay Commission recommendations, but directed that no recovery of the excess amount be made as they were not at fault.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 11) “11. Although we have held that the petitioners were entitled only to the pay scale of Rs 330-480 in terms of the recommendations of the Third Pay Commission w.e.f. January 1, 1973 and only after the period of 10 years, they became entitled to the pay scale of Rs 330-560 but as they have received the scale of Rs 330-560 since 1973 due to no fault of theirs and that scale is being reduced in the year 1984 with effect from January 1, 1973, it shall only be just and proper not to recover any excess amount which has already been paid to them”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Early authority for no recovery of excess pay received without fault of the employee.

### 7. Dr. Ravindra s/o Shamrao Darunte v. State of Maharashtra and others

- **File:** `BHC_2017-03-10_Ravindra-Shamrao-Darunte-v-State-of-Maharashtra.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Bombay High Court, Bench at Aurangabad; Writ Petition No. 6191 of 2016; 10.03.2017
- **Bench:** T.V. Nalawade and Sangitrao S. Patil, JJ.
- **Citation:** Citation not shown in the source copy
- **Facts:** A Medical Officer under the Rural Development Department retired on 31.07.2015; recovery of excess payment due to wrong pay fixation was ordered by orders dated 23.12.2015 and 12.05.2016. He had given an undertaking to refund excess payment when opting for the revised pay scale.
- **Holding:** Petition dismissed. The Court applied Rafiq Masih and Jagdev Singh (undertaking), noted the petition did not plead hardship, and relied on Rule 134-A of the Maharashtra Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1982 (recovery and adjustment of excess amount paid).
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 6) “6. In the present case also, the petitioner, who was a Medical Officer, has given an undertaking while opting for the revised pay scale that he would refund the excess amount, if any, in case it is noticed that it was paid to him because of wrong pay fixation. There is nothing in the petition that because of recovery of the excess payment made to him, he would be put to suffer any hardship. It is common knowledge that the Medical Officers, after retirement start private medical practice. The petitioner has not disclosed any impediment in his way in starting private medical practice. The possibility of his earning by private medical practice cannot be ruled out. In fact, it was necessary for the petitioner to make out a specific case in the petition itself by pleading his financial condition and resultant hardship to which he would be subjected in the event of recovery of excess payment made to him. The petition is totally salient on this vital ground, which was one of the considerations before the Hon'ble Supreme Court in the case of State of Punjab vs. Rafiq Masih (supra), for banning recovery of excess payment made to the employees, who are retired or on the verge of retirement.”
  - (para 8 (Rule 134-A as reproduced)) “Rule 134-A of the Pension Rules, 1982, reads as under :- "134-A-Recovery and adjustment of excess amount paid - If in the case of a Government servant, who has retired or has been allowed to retire, (i) it is found that due to any reason whatsoever an excess amount has been paid to him during the period of his service including service rendered upon re- employment after retirement, or (ii) any amount is found to be payable by the pensioner during such period and which has not been paid by or recovered from him, or (iii)it is found the amount of licence fee and any other dues pertaining to Government accommodation is recoverable from him for the occupation of the Government accommodation after the retirement. then the excess amount so paid, the amount so found payable or recoverable shall be recovered from the amount of pension sanctioned to him. Provided that, the Government shall give a reasonable opportunity to the pensioner to show cause as to why the amount due should not be recovered from him: Provided further that, the amount found due may be recovered from the pensioner in installment so that the amount of pension is not reduced below the minimum fixed by Government.)”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Maharashtra authority applying Rule 134-A MCS (Pension) Rules, 1982 and an undertaking against a non-Class III/IV retiree; read with Nilam Naik.

### 8. Smt. Nilam Shripad Naik v. The Registrar General, High Court, Appellate Side, Mumbai and others

- **File:** `BHC_2019-03-08_Nilam-Shripad-Naik-v-Registrar-General-High-Court.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Bombay High Court (Ordinary Original Civil Jurisdiction); Writ Petition No. 3128 of 2018; 08.03.2019
- **Bench:** B.R. Gavai and N.J. Jamadar, JJ.
- **Citation:** Citation not shown in the source copy
- **Facts:** A retired High Court employee (Section Officer, described by the respondents as Class-II) challenged recovery of Rs. 1,44,834/- from her Death-cum-Retirement Gratuity, a couple of days before retirement, on account of excess pay fixation from July 2010 (increment released contrary to GR dated 05.05.2010). The respondents relied on undertakings and Jagdev Singh.
- **Holding:** Court found re-fixation correct but the recovery from DCRG iniquitous: no undertaking existed for the particular fixation, excess payment continued for more than five years (proposition (iii)), hardship (proposition (v)); the undertaking dated 22.01.2018 obtained on the verge of retirement was of no consequence. Refund of Rs. 1,44,834/- directed within two months.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 18 (extract)) “18. In the case at hand, it is pertinent to note that the mistake in determination of the pay, admittedly, occurred on 1st July, 2010. The said mistake occurred subsequent to the non-functional pay scale having been made applicable to the Petitioner with effect from 4th February, 2010. The Respondents have not placed on record any document to show that any undertaking was furnished by the Petitioner before or immediately after the said non-functional pay scale was made applicable to the Petitioner. The first undertaking dated 5 th September, 2009 was in the nature of an accompaniment to the Government Circular dated 29th April, 2009. The second undertaking dated 2nd May, 2014 was on account of fixation of pay consequent to the promotion of the Petitioner to the post of 'Assistant Registrar'. The undertaking dated 29 th August, 2018 was pursuant to the fixation of pay in the pay scale in terms of Government Resolution dated 31st March, 2018, post retirement. What is conspicuous by its absence is such undertaking in the context of the application of non-functional pay scale with effect from 4th February, 2010.”
  - (para 22) “22. We are, therefore, of the view that the recovery of the amount of Rs.1,44,834/- from the Death-Cum-Retirement Gratuity, payable to the Petitioner, a couple of days prior to her retirement was iniquitous and unjustifiable. In our view, the undertaking dated 22nd January, 2018 is of little assistance to the Respondents. Though styled as an undertaking, the said document was, in effect, in the nature of her consent for the recovery from admissible Death-Cum-Retirement Gratuity benefit. Faced with the prospect of either loosing out, or inordinate delay in getting, the retiral benefit, such consent can hardly be said to be of the Petitioner's own volition and freewill, so as to bind her inexorably. An undertaking obtained by an employer from an employee at the verge of her retirement when the employee has no bargaining power, in our view, would be of no consequence.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Bombay High Court on undertakings obtained at the verge of retirement and on propositions (ii), (iii), (v) of Rafiq Masih for judiciary staff.


## B. Medical reimbursement

### 9. State of Punjab and others v. Ram Lubhaya Bagga etc.

- **File:** `SC_1998-02-26_State-of-Punjab-v-Ram-Lubhaya-Bagga.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeals arising out of SLP(C) Nos. 13167, 12418, 12143, 12144 and 11968 of 1997; 26.02.1998
- **Bench:** S.B. Majmudar, M. Jagannadha Rao and A.P. Misra, JJ.
- **Citation:** (1998) 4 SCC 117; AIR 1998 SC 1703
- **Facts:** Punjab Government employees/pensioners claimed full reimbursement of heart-surgery expenses at Escorts Hospital (non-Government); the State's policy dated 13.02.1995 limited reimbursement to AIIMS rates.
- **Holding:** The new policy dated 13.02.1995 upheld as not violating Article 21; reimbursement at AIIMS rates; some respondents allowed difference on their facts, not to be treated as precedent.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (unnumbered paragraph (decision)) “We having considered the submission of both the parties, on the aforesaid facts and circumstances, hold that the appellant's decision to exclude the designated hospital cannot be said be such as to be violative of Article 21 of the Constitution. No right could be absolute in a welfare State. A man is a social animal. He cannot live without the cooperation of large number of persons. Every article one uses is the contribution of many. Hence every individual right has to give way to the right of public at large. Not every fundamental right under Part III of the Constitution is not absolute and it is o be within permissible reasonable restriction. This principle equally applies when there is any constraint on the health budget on account of financial stringencies. But we do hope that government will give due consideration and priority to the health budget in future and render what is best possible.”
  - (unnumbered paragraph (conclusion)) “For the aforesaid reasons and findings we uphold governments new policy dated 13th February, 1995 and further hold it not to be violative of Article 21 of the Constitution of India.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Limits of medical reimbursement policy (rates, empanelment) and the financial-constraint reasoning for upholding a restricted policy.

### 10. State of Punjab and others v. Mohinder Singh Chawla etc.

- **File:** `SC_1996-12-17_State-of-Punjab-v-Mohinder-Singh-Chawla.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal No. 16979 of 1996 (SLP(C) No. 12472 of 1996) with CA Nos. 16980-81 of 1996; 17.12.1996
- **Bench:** K. Ramaswamy and G.B. Pattanaik, JJ.
- **Citation:** (1997) 2 SCC 83; AIR 1997 SC 1225
- **Facts:** Punjab Government employee underwent valve replacement at AIIMS Delhi on permission of the Medical Board; the State disallowed hospital room rent under a Resolution dated 25.01.1991; the High Court directed reimbursement.
- **Holding:** Expenses of in-patient stay form an integral part of the treatment expenses; right to health is integral to the right to life; the State must reimburse. Appeal dismissed.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (unnumbered paragraph) “It is now settled law that right to health is an integral to right to life. Government has constitutional obligation to provide the health facilities. If the Government servant has suffered an ailment which requires treatment at a specialised approved hospital and on reference whereat the Government servant had undergone such treatment therein, it is but the duty of the State to bear the expenditure incurred by the Government servant. Expenditure, thus, incurred requires to be reimbursed by the State to the employee.”
  - (unnumbered paragraph) “Having had the constitutional obligation to bear the expenses for the Government servant while in service or after retirement from service, as per the policy of the Government, the government is required to fulfill the constitutional obligation. Necessarily, the State has to bear the expenses incurred in that behalf.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Reimbursement of in-patient expenses for approved specialised treatment; cited in Ram Lubhaya Bagga.

### 11. Shiva Kant Jha v. Union of India

- **File:** `SC_2018-04-13_Shiva-Kant-Jha-v-Union-of-India.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India (Civil Original Jurisdiction); Writ Petition (Civil) No. 694 of 2015; 13.04.2018
- **Bench:** R.K. Agrawal and Ashok Bhushan, JJ.
- **Citation:** AIR 2018 SC 1975; (2018) 5 SCALE 551
- **Facts:** A retired CGHS pensioner's medical bills for emergency treatment at Fortis Escorts Hospital, Delhi and Jaslok Hospital, Mumbai (non-empanelled) were partly rejected, including on the ground that prior approval for the device implant was not sought.
- **Holding:** Treatment in non-empanelled hospitals in emergency held genuine; balance of Rs. 4,99,555/- directed to be paid (decision confined to the case); Secretary-level committee and time limits directed for pensioners' medical claims.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 13) “13) It is a settled legal position that the Government employee during his life time or after his retirement is entitled to get the benefit of the medical facilities and no fetters can be placed on his rights. It is acceptable to common sense, that ultimate decision as to how a patient should be treated vests only with the Doctor, who is well versed and expert both on academic qualification and experience gained. Very little scope is left to the patient or his relative to decide as to the manner in which the ailment should be treated. Speciality Hospitals are established for treatment of specified ailments and services of Doctors specialized in a discipline are availed by patients only to ensure proper, required and safe treatment. Can it be said that taking treatment in Speciality Hospital by itself would deprive a person to claim reimbursement solely on the ground that the said Hospital is not included in the Government Order. The right to medical claim cannot be denied merely because the name of the hospital is not included in the Government Order. The real test must be the factum of treatment. Before any medical claim is honoured, the authorities are bound to ensure as to whether the claimant had actually taken treatment and the factum of treatment is supported by records duly certified by Doctors/Hospitals concerned. Once, it is established, the claim cannot be denied on technical grounds.”
  - (para 15) “15) In the present view of the matter, we are of the considered opinion that the CGHS is responsible for taking care of healthcare needs and well being of the central government employees and pensioners. In the facts and circumstances of the case, we are of opinion that the treatment of the petitioner in non-empanelled hospital was genuine because there was no option left with him at the relevant time. We, therefore, direct the respondent-State to pay the balance amount of Rs. 4,99,555/- to the writ petitioner. We also make it clear that the said decision is confined to this case only.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Reimbursement for emergency treatment in non-empanelled hospitals; factum of treatment as the test.


## C. Compassionate appointment

### 12. Umesh Kumar Nagpal v. State of Haryana and others

- **File:** `SC_1994-05-04_Umesh-Kumar-Nagpal-v-State-of-Haryana.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Special Leave Petitions against LPA No. 734 of 1992 and CWP No. 6357 of 1992 (P&H High Court); numbers not shown in the source copy; 04.05.1994
- **Bench:** P.B. Sawant and N.P. Singh, JJ.
- **Citation:** (1994) 4 SCC 138
- **Facts:** Petitions challenged the decision dated 18.12.1992 of the Punjab and Haryana High Court Division Bench on compassionate appointment; dismissed at admission with a short judgment on the guiding considerations.
- **Holding:** Compassionate appointment is an exception to the rule of open recruitment; the object is to enable the family to tide over the sudden crisis; Government must examine the financial condition of the family; only Class III and IV posts.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 2) “The whole object of granting compassionate employment is thus to enable the family to tide over the sudden crisis. The object is not to give a member of such family a post much less a post for post held by the deceased.”
  - (para 2) “The Government or the public authority concerned has to examine the financial condition of the family of the deceased, and it is only if it is satisfied, that but for the provision of employment, the family will not be able to meet the crisis that a job is to be offered to the eligible member of the family.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Foundational authority on the object and limits of compassionate appointment.

### 13. State of Himachal Pradesh and another v. Shashi Kumar

- **File:** `SC_2019-01-16_State-of-HP-v-Shashi-Kumar.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal No. 988 of 2019 (arising out of SLP(C) No. 7079 of 2016); 16.01.2019
- **Bench:** Dr. D.Y. Chandrachud and Hemant Gupta, JJ.
- **Citation:** (2019) 3 SCC 653
- **Facts:** Respondent applied on 08.05.2007 for compassionate appointment; the State took into account family pension and fixed an income limit through Finance Department letters; High Court directed reconsideration without counting family pension.
- **Holding:** Fixation of income limit upheld as a policy matter (to be revised periodically); family pension may be taken into account; claim also barred by delay of over seven years; High Court directions set aside.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (unnumbered paragraph) “What should be the appropriate income criterion is undoubtedly a matter of policy for the State Government to determine. However, we would impress upon the State Government the need to periodically revise the income limits preferably at intervals of three years. Inflation and the increase in the cost of living have an important bearing on financial exigencies faced by families of serving as well as deceased employees. In fixing the income criteria for considering cases of compassionate appointment, it would be appropriate if the State revisits the income limit at periodic intervals, as we have indicated above. We clarify that it would be open to the State to revise the income limits at a frequency of less than three years, if the State is so advised.”

## G. Delay, laches and limitation of arrears

### 23. Union of India and another v. Tarsem Singh

- **File:** `SC_2008-08-13_Union-of-India-v-Tarsem-Singh.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal Nos. 5151-5152 of 2008 (arising out of SLP(C) Nos. 3820-3821 of 2008); 13.08.2008
- **Bench:** Lokeshwar Singh Panta and R.V. Raveendran, JJ.
- **Citation:** (2008) 8 SCC 648
- **Facts:** An Army man invalidated out of service on 13.11.1983 sought disability pension in the High Court in 1999; the Single Judge limited arrears to 38 months before filing; the Division Bench directed payment from the date it fell due (16 years) with 6% interest.
- **Holding:** Belated service claim is normally refused on delay and laches; continuing wrong is an exception for pay/pension re-fixation; but arrears are restricted to three years before filing of the writ petition; interest not granted.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 5) “5. To summarise, normally, a belated service related claim will be rejected on the ground of delay and laches (where remedy is sought by filing a writ petition) or limitation (where remedy is sought by an application to the Administrative Tribunal). One of the exceptions to the said rule is cases relating to a continuing wrong. Where a service related claim is based on a continuing wrong, relief can be granted even if there is a long delay in seeking remedy, with reference to the date on which the continuing wrong commenced, if such continuing wrong creates a continuing source of injury. But there is an exception to the exception. If the grievance is in respect of any order or administrative decision which related to or affected several others also, and if the re-opening of the issue would affect the settled rights of third parties, then the claim will not be entertained. For example, if the issue relates to payment or re-fixation of pay or pension, relief may be granted in spite of delay as it does not affect the rights of third parties. But if the claim involved issues relating to seniority or promotion etc., affecting others, delay would render the claim stale and doctrine of laches/limitation will be applied. In so far as the consequential relief of recovery of arrears for a past period, the principles relating to recurring/successive wrongs will apply. As a consequence, High Courts will restrict the consequential relief relating to arrears normally to a period of three years prior to the date of filing of the writ petition.”
  - (para 6) “6. In this case, the delay of 16 years would affect the consequential claim for arrears. The High Court was not justified in directing payment of arrears relating to 16 years, and that too with interest. It ought to have restricted the relief relating to arrears to only three years before the date of writ petition, or from the date of demand to date of writ petition, whichever was lesser. It ought not to have granted interest on arrears in such circumstances.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Limitation on arrears of pay/pension claims (three years before the petition); continuing wrong.

(1) wherein this Court authoritatively ruled that pension is a right and the payment of it does not depend upon the discretion of the Government but is governed by the rules and a Government servant coming within those rules is entitled to claim pension. It was further held that the grant of pension does not depend upon any one's discretion.”
  - (unnumbered paragraph (conclusion)) “we are satisfied that by introducing an arbitrary eligibility criteria: 'being in service and retiring subsequent to the specified date' for being eligible for the liberalised pension scheme and thereby dividing a homogeneous class, the classification being not based on any discernible rational principle and having been found wholly unrelated to the objects sought to be achieved by grant of liberalised pension and the eligibility criteria devised being thoroughly arbitrary, we are of the view that the eligibility for liberalised pension scheme of being in service on the specified date and retiring subsequent to that date' in impugned memoranda, Exhibits P-I and P-2, violates Art. 14 and is unconstitutional and is struck down.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Pension is a right; cut-off date classification among pensioners tested under Article 14.

### 29. P.U. Joshi and others v. The Accountant General, Ahmedabad and others (with Union of India v. Basudeba Dora and others)

- **File:** `SC_2002-12-19_PU-Joshi-v-Accountant-General-Ahmedabad.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal Nos. 4679-4680 of 1996 and 10983 of 1996; 19.12.2002
- **Bench:** Doraiswamy Raju and Shivaraj V. Patil, JJ.
- **Citation:** (2003) 2 SCC 632; AIR 2003 SC 2156
- **Facts:** Supervisors and Section Officers in the Indian Audit and Accounts Department challenged the effect of the bifurcation of 01.03.1984 and the restructuring of cadres and categories in the audit offices.
- **Holding:** Matters of cadres, qualifications and service conditions lie in the policy field of the State; employees have no right that service rules remain forever the same, save for rights already accrued; claims dismissed.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (unnumbered paragraph) “Questions relating to the constitution, pattern, nomenclature of posts, cadres, categories, their creation/abolition, prescription of qualifications and other conditions of service including avenues of promotions and criteria to be fulfilled for such promotions pertain to the field of Policy and within the exclusive discretion and jurisdiction of the State, subject, of course, to the limitations or restrictions envisaged in the Constitution of India and it is not for the Statutory Tribunals, at any rate, to direct the Government to have a particular method of recruitment or eligibility criteria or avenues of promotion or impose itself by substituting its views for that of the State. Similarly, it is well open and within the competency of the State to change the rules relating to a service and alter or amend and vary by addition/substruction the qualifications, eligibility criteria and other conditions of service including avenues of promotion, from time to time, as the administrative exigencies may need or necessitate. Likewise, the State by appropriate rules is entitled to amalgamate departments or bifurcate departments into more and constitute different categories of posts or cadres by undertaking further classification, bifurcation or amalgamation as well as reconstitute and restructure the pattern and cadres/categories of service, as may be required from time to time by abolishing existing cadres/posts and creating new cadres/posts. There is no right in any employee of the State to claim that rules governing conditions of his service should be forever the same as the one when he entered service for all purposes and except for ensuring or safeguarding rights or benefits already earned, acquired or accrued at a particular point of time, a Government servant has no right to challenge the authority of the State to amend, alter and bring into force new rules relating to even an existing service.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Employer's power to change service conditions prospectively (relevant to transition from the old pension scheme to a new scheme).

### 30. Shivaji Nagnath Lokare and others v. State of Maharashtra and others

- **Bench:** S.C. Dharmadhikari and B.P. Colabawalla, JJ.
- **Citation:** (2017) 5 Mh.L.J. 854 (as cited in Riyaz Ahmad Sadroddin, 2023)
- **Facts:** 51 Gram Sevaks of Solapur Zilla Parishad (selection dates 10.09.2005 to 28.10.2005; contract appointment 16.11.2005; confirmed 16.11.2008) sought continuation of the MCS (Pension) Rules, 1982 and GPF instead of DCPS under GR dated 31.10.2005.
- **Holding:** Writ petition dismissed; Court distinguished 'selection/recruitment' from 'appointment' relying on the Supreme Court's reading of Article 233(2); GRs dated 31.10.2005, 21.05.2010 and 19.07.2011 not set aside.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 12 (part)) “By the first Resolution dated 31st October, 2005, the Government of Maharashtra declared that this new pension scheme would be made available by replacing the existing pension scheme and would be applicable to the Government servants who are recruited on or after 1 st November, 2005. Sub-clause (c) of clause 2 of the first resolution records the decision of the Government that the provisions of the Maharashtra Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1982, and the Maharashtra Civil Services (Commutation of Pension) Rules, 1982, and the existing General Provident Fund Scheme would not be applicable to the Government servants who are recruited on or after 1st November, 2005.”
  - (para 25) “25 Given this binding mandate and flowing from this decision and even otherwise in the facts and circumstances of the present case, we do not find that the Zilla Parishad was wrong or in error in issuing the impugned communication. They are based on the Government Resolutions. The Government Resolutions themselves cannot be set aside and at the instance of such of the petitioners before us who are relying upon a process prior to the appointment, but culminating in the appointment.”
  - (para 27) “27 As a result of the above discussion, we do not find any merit in the writ petition. Rule is discharged.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** DCPS applicability based on appointment date; cited in Khilari Rajendra Eknath (2023) and Riyaz Ahmad Sadroddin (2023).

### 31. Khilari Rajendra Eknath and others v. State of Maharashtra and others

- **File:** `BHC_2023-04-28_Khilari-Rajendra-Eknath-v-State-of-Maharashtra.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Bombay High Court (Ordinary Original Civil Jurisdiction); Writ Petition No. 2270 of 2021; 28.04.2023
- **Bench:** S.V. Gangapurwala, Acting CJ and Sandeep V. Marne, J.
- **Citation:** 2023:BHC-OS:3857-DB
- **Facts:** 56 Clerks of the High Court (Appellate Side) whose recruitment advertisement was issued on 20.06.2005 (written test 09.08.2005, typing test before 01.11.2005) but who were appointed on 28.12.2005 sought the Old Pension Scheme and GPF instead of DCPS/NPS under GR dated 31.10.2005 and the High Court Notification dated 21.04.2008.
- **Holding:** Writ petition allowed: Rule 2(2) of the MCS (Pension) Rules, 1982 uses 'recruited', not 'appointed'; petitioners declared governed by the Old Pension Scheme and GPF; DCPS contributions to be credited to GPF; State expected to issue an OM giving a one-time option like the Central Government OM dated 03.03.2023.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 27 to 29) “27. Sub-Rule (2) of Rule 2 of the M.C.S. (Pension) Rules, 1982 states that these rules would not apply to the Government Servants who are recruited on or after 01.11.2005. Rules 1982 are the beneficial piece of legislation governing the post retiral benefits to a government employee. It has been held time and again that pension is not a bounty. Rules 1982 are not made applicable to the government servants who are recruited on or after 01.11.2005. Though the amendment to the Rules 1982 by the State Government are based upon the amendment carried out in the Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972 there is a subtle difference between the two. The Central Civil Services (Pension) Rules, 1972 apply to the Government Servants appointed on or before 31.12.2003, Mohite 20/23 meaning thereby that it would not apply to the government servants appointed on or after 1st day of January, 2004. Whereas under the said M.C.S. (Pension) Rules, 1982, the wordings are 'the Rules shall not apply to government servants who are recruited on or after 01.11.2005'. The phraseology "Recruitment" and "Appointment" are not synonymous. There is a marked distinction between "Recruitment" and "Appointment". Recruitment is a stage prior to appointment. Recruitment is the process which entails selection of candidates. Selection is a process of picking the candidates from the short listed candidates. An appointment means an actual act of posting a person to a particular office. 28. The Apex Court in the case of Shivaji Nagnath Lokare and Others (Supra) has observed that the appointment cannot be read to include the word 'selection, recruitment or recruitment process'. The Apex Court in case of Prafulla Kumar Swain Etc. vs. Prakash Chandra Misra And Ors. 8 observed that recruitment cannot tantamount to appointment. 29. In the present case, the advertisement was issued on 20.06.2005 and the last date for submission of Application was 11.07.2005. The written test was also conducted on 09.08.2005 which was cleared by the Petitioner. They also cleared typing test prior to 01.11.2005. The appointment order is issued subsequently. The legislature in its wisdom has not used the term "appointment on or after 01.11.2005" in Sub-Rule (2) of Rule 2 of the M.C.S. (Pension) Rules, 1982. The M.C.S. (Pension) Rules, 1982 would not apply to the persons who are recruited on or after 01.11.2005 meaning thereby that the said Rule would not apply to the persons 8 1993 SCC Supl. (3) 181 Mohite 21/23 whose recruitment process had commenced after 01.11.2005. The M.C.S. (Pension) Rules, 1982 shall apply to persons recruitted in government services prior to 01.11.2005 though appointment orders are issued subsequent to 01.11.2005.”
  - (paras 30 and 31) “30. Resultantly, the Writ Petition succeeds. It is declared that Petitioners shall be governed by the provisions of the Old Pension Scheme in vogue prior to 1.11.2005, as well as General Provident Fund Scheme. Petitioners shall not be governed by the provisions of the Defined Contributory Pension Scheme introduced vide G.R. dated 31.10.2005. The Petitioners' contribution to DCPS be credited to their GPF Accounts. The modalities as suggested in para- 8 of the Office Memorandum dated 03.03.2023 issued by the Government of India be adopted while switching over Petitioners from DCPS to Old Pension Scheme. 31. We hope and trust that the State of Maharashtra would issue Office Memorandum similar to Office Memorandum dated 03.03.2023 issued by the Central Government clarifying that in all cases where State Government employee has been appointed against a post or vacancy which was advertised/notified for recruitment/appointment prior to the date of notification for Defined Contributory Pension Scheme i.e. 31.10.2005 and is covered under the Defined Contributory Pension Scheme on joining service on after 01.11.2005, may be given one time option to be covered under the The M.C.S. (Pension) Rules, 1982. Such an office memorandum would avoid further litigation.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Primary Bombay High Court authority on old pension scheme vs DCPS/NPS where recruitment process began before 01.11.2005 and appointment followed.

### 32. Deshmukh Dilipkumar Bhagwan and others v. State of Maharashtra and others (Full Bench reference)

- **File:** `BHC_2019-04-30_Deshmukh-Dilipkumar-Bhagwan-v-State-of-Maharashtra-FullBench.txt`
- **Court / case no. / date:** Bombay High Court (Full Bench, Civil Appellate Jurisdiction); Writ Petition No. 8387 of 2013 with connected petitions; 30.04.2019
- **Bench:** S.C. Dharmadhikari, Akil Kureshi and Nitin W. Sambre, JJ.
- **Citation:** AIRONLINE 2019 BOM 1508
- **Facts:** Reference to a larger Bench of three questions on old pension scheme vs DCP scheme for employees of aided private schools and colleges of education appointed before 01.11.2005 in institutions receiving less than 100% grant-in-aid.
- **Holding:** Only institutions receiving 100% grant-in-aid are 'aided' for pension liability; employees appointed before 01.11.2005 in institutions receiving less than 100% grant on 01.11.2005 (or which became 100% aided before 29.11.2010) are governed by the DCP scheme; no vested right is taken away.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - (para 36) “36. The petitioners had also argued that right of retired Government servants to receive pension is a vested right. It is neither bounty nor a largesse to be given by the employer. To this propostion, there can be no quarrel. However, right to receive post-retiral benefits flow from the scheme provided by the employer. In the present case, as discussed earlier, the existing pension scheme is replaced by new pension scheme. No vested right of the employees is being taken away.”
  - (para 37 (Question No. 2)) “The employees who were appointed prior to 1.11.2005 in aided recognized primary, secondary schools as well as colleges of education which were receiving less than 100% grant-in-aid as on 1.11.2005 would be governed by the DCP scheme.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** DCP scheme and aided institutions; replacement of the existing pension scheme does not take away a vested right.
