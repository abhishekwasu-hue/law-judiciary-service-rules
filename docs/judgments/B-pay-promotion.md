## Randhir Singh v. Union of India (1982-02-22)
- **File:** `SC_1982-02-22_Randhir-Singh-v-UoI.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; Writ Petition No. 4676 of 1978
- **Citation:** (1982) 1 SCC 618; AIR 1982 SC 879
- **Bench:** O. Chinnappa Reddy, A.P. Sen, Baharul Islam, JJ.
- **Topic:** Pay parity
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1230349/
- **Facts:** Driver-constable of the Delhi Police Force, on a lower pay scale than drivers of other departments (Railway Protection Force), sought equal pay for equal work (Writ Petition 4676/1978).
- **Holding:** Equal pay for equal work, though not expressly a fundamental right, is a constitutional goal deducible from Articles 14, 16 and 39(d) and can be enforced where unequal scales rest on no classification or an irrational classification; belonging to different departments is no answer. Petition allowed.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *(unnumbered):* “It is true that the principle of 'equal pay for equal work' is not expressly declared by our Constitution to be a fundamental right. But it certainly is a Constitutional goal. Art. 39(d) of the Constitution proclaims 'equal pay for equal work for both men and women" as a Directive Principle of State Policy. 'Equal pay for equal work for both men and women' means equal pay for equal work for everyone and as between the sexes. Directive principles, as has been pointed out in some of the judgments of this Court have to be read into the fundamental rights as a matter of interpretation. Art. 14 of the Constitution enjoins the state not to deny any person equality before the law or the equal protection of the laws and Art. 16 declares that there shall be equality of opportunity for all citizens in matters relating to employment or appointment to any office under 305 the State. These equality clauses of the Constitution must mean some thing to everyone. To the vast majority of the people the equality clauses of the Constitution would mean nothing if they are unconcerned with the work they do and the pay they get. To them the equality clauses will have some substance if equal work means equal pay.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Foundational authority for equal pay for equal work in service matters.

## State of U.P. v. J.P. Chaurasia (1988-09-27)
- **File:** `SC_1988-09-27_State-of-UP-v-JP-Chaurasia.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeal No. 56 of 1988 (as in source header)
- **Citation:** (1989) 1 SCC 121; AIR 1989 SC 19
- **Bench:** K.J. Shetty, G.L. Oza, JJ.
- **Topic:** Pay parity / higher pay scale (High Court staff)
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1877922/
- **Facts:** Bench Secretaries Grade I of the Allahabad High Court (selected on merit with regard to seniority) were on a higher pay scale than Bench Secretaries Grade II; the High Court granted the higher scale to Grade II on equal pay for equal work.
- **Holding:** Appeal allowed. Equal pay for equal work has no mechanical application; classification based on experience/merit for a higher scale within the same cadre is reasonable and has a rational nexus; fixing of pay scales is for the administration.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *(unnumbered):* “The differentiation so made in the same cadre will not amount to discrimination. The classification based on experience is a reasonable classification. It has a rational nexus with the object thereof. To hold otherwise, it would be detrimental to the interest of the service itself.”
  - *(unnumbered):* “In the present case, all Bench Secretaries may do the same work, but their quality of work may differ. Under the rules framed by the Chief Justice of the High Court, Bench Secretaries Grade I are selected by a Selection Committee. The selection is based on merit with due regard to seniority. They are selected among the lot of Bench Secretaries Grade II. When Bench Secretaries Grade II acquire experience and also display more merit, they are appointed as Bench Secretaries Grade I. The rules thus make a proper classification for the purpose of entitlement to higher pay scale. The High Court has completely overlooked the criterion provided under the Rules The merit governs the grant of higher pay scale and that merit will be evaluated by a competent authority. The classification made under the Rules, therefore, cannot be said to be violative of the right to have equal pay for equal work.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Cite where staff in the same cadre are placed on a higher scale on merit-cum-seniority or experience, and a claim of parity is made.

## Mewa Ram Kanojia v. All India Institute of Medical Sciences (1989-03-09)
- **File:** `SC_1989-03-09_Mewa-Ram-Kanojia-v-AIIMS.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; Writ Petition (Civil) No. 4611 of 1983 (as in source header)
- **Citation:** AIR 1989 SC 1256; 1989 (1) SCR 957
- **Bench:** K.N. Singh, K.J. Shetty, JJ.
- **Topic:** Pay parity
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/297577/
- **Facts:** Hearing Therapist at AIIMS claimed the pay scale of Speech Therapist/Audiologist under equal pay for equal work (Writ Petition under Article 32).
- **Holding:** Petition dismissed. Equal pay for equal work requires functional equality with qualitative sameness; qualification differences justify different scales; Pay Commission's value judgment not interfered with; no comparison where the employer is different.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *(unnumbered):* “Moreover, if the employer is not the same the principle of 'Equal Pay for Equal Work' would not be applicable. We do not consider it necessary to discuss the matter further as the petitioner has not placed requisite material before the Court for the application of the principle of 'Equal Pay for Equal Work'. In view of the above discussion we are of the opinion that the petitioner has failed to demonstrate that any discrimination has been practised against him in the matter relating to pay, therefore the question of application of the principle of 'Equal Pay for Equal Work' does not arise and the petitioner is not entitled to any relief.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Cite for the limits of the equal pay principle (different qualifications, different employer, Pay Commission's assessment).

## Direct Recruit Class II Engineering Officers' Association v. State of Maharashtra (1990-05-02)
- **File:** `SC_1990-05-02_Direct-Recruit-Class-II-Engineers-v-State-of-Maharashtra.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeals (and Writ Petitions) - see source header
- **Citation:** AIR 1990 SC 1607; 1990 (2) SCR 900
- **Bench:** L.M. Sharma, K. Ramaswamy, Sabyasachi Mukharji, S.R. Pandian, P.B. Sawant, JJ. (Constitution Bench)
- **Topic:** Seniority
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/485116/
- **Facts:** Dispute between direct recruits and promotee Deputy Engineers of the Maharashtra Engineering Service over inter se seniority, quota rule and counting of officiating service.
- **Holding:** Seniority of an incumbent appointed according to rule is counted from the date of appointment, not confirmation; ad hoc/stop-gap officiation not according to rules is not counted, but uninterrupted officiation followed by regularisation is counted; quota rule breakdown principles laid down.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *para 44 (To sum up: (A), (B)):* “(A) Once an incumbent is appointed to a post according to rule, his seniority has to be counted from the date of his appointment and not according to the date of his confirma- tion. The corollary of the above rule is that where the initial appointment is only ad hoc and not according to rules and made as a stop-gap arrangement, the officiation in such post cannot be taken into account for considering the seniority. (B) If the initial appointment is not made by following the procedure laid down by the rules but the appointee continues in the post uninterruptedly till the regularisa- tion of his service in accordance with the rules, the period of officiating service will be counted.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Cite for the principle on counting officiating service in seniority and for the date from which seniority runs.

## Union of India v. K.V. Jankiraman (1991-08-27)
- **File:** `SC_1991-08-27_UoI-v-KV-Jankiraman.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; Civil Appeals Nos. 3019/87 etc.
- **Citation:** (1991) 4 SCC 109; AIR 1991 SC 2010
- **Bench:** P.B. Sawant, Rangnath Misra, M.H. Kania, JJ.
- **Topic:** Notional promotion / arrears (sealed cover)
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1142043/
- **Facts:** Employees against whom disciplinary/criminal proceedings were pending had promotion kept in sealed cover; on exoneration they claimed promotion from the date juniors were promoted and arrears of pay.
- **Holding:** Normal rule of 'no work no pay' does not apply where the employee was willing to work but was kept away for no fault of his; where the employee is exonerated, the sealed cover is opened and promotion/benefits follow from the due date; whether arrears are payable for the period of notional promotion is to be decided by the authority on the facts, with reasons if denied.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *para 7:* “The normal rule of "no work no pay" is not applicable to cases such as the present one where the employee although he is willing to work is kept away from work by the authorities for no fault of his.”
  - *para 7:* “However, whether the officer concerned will be entitled to any arrears of pay for the period of notional promotion preceding the date of actual promotion, and if so to what extent, will be decided by the concerned authority by taking into consideration all the facts and circumstances of the disciplinary proceeding/criminal prosecution. Where the authority denies arrears of salary or part of it, it will record its reasons for doing so.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Cite for arrears of pay on notional/deemed promotion where the employee was prevented from working for no fault of his.

## All India Judges' Association v. Union of India (I) (1991-11-13)
- **File:** `SC_1991-11-13_AIJA-v-UoI-I.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; Writ Petition (Civil) No. 1022 of 1989
- **Citation:** (1992) 1 SCC 119; AIR 1992 SC 165
- **Bench:** Ranganath Misra CJ, A.M. Ahmadi, P.B. Sawant, JJ.
- **Topic:** Judicial officers' pay
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1394975/
- **Facts:** Writ petition (C) 1022/1989 under Article 32 by the All India Judges' Association seeking an All India Judicial Service and uniform service conditions, including pay scales, for the subordinate judiciary.
- **Holding:** The Court did not fix pay scales finally (insufficient data) but directed that whenever pay commissions/committees are set up in the States and Union Territories, they separately examine and review the pay structure of judicial officers; uniform designations directed; allowances and other service conditions dealt with.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *Part IV (pay scales; unnumbered):* “It is difficult for us on the data now placed to get into the exercise of fixing the appropri- ate pay scales. We suffer a handicap in the absence of full details necessary for fixing the appropriate pay scales on comparative basis. Again, we are apprehensing that if we enter into the matter and say something in a final way, it is possible that in some States benefits which are now available may be taken away or adversely affect some offi- cers. For these reasons, we do not propose to finally exam- ine the propriety of the existing pay scales nor do we direct any pay scales to be fixed. A Pay Commission for the Central Government employees was appointed about 8 years back and on the basis of its Report the revised benefits have been given effect to from January 1, 1986. Following that pattern, most of the States have either given the Central scales or appointed their own commissions or committees and given the revised benefits to their officers. It appears that with an interval of 10 years or so such a commission is being appointed and pay scales are being reviewed. Such an exercise is likely to be under- taken within less than three or four years. We are of the view that the claim on this score can be better handled when the pay commissions or committees in the States are set up to review the position. We direct that as and when such commissions or committees are set up in the States and Union Territories hereafter, they separately examine and review the pay structure of judicial officers keeping in view all relevant aspects.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Origin of the direction that State pay commissions must separately review the pay structure of judicial officers (basis of later Shetty and Padmanabhan commissions).

## All India Judges' Association v. Union of India (II) (review) (1993-08-24)
- **File:** `SC_1993-08-24_AIJA-v-UoI-II.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; Writ Petition (Civil) No. 1022 of 1989
- **Citation:** (1993) 4 SCC 288; AIR 1993 SC 2493
- **Bench:** M.N. Venkatachaliah, A.M. Ahmadi, P.B. Sawant, JJ.
- **Topic:** Judicial officers' pay
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1977799/
- **Facts:** Review petitions by Union and States against the 1991 directions, pleading financial burden and State autonomy in respect of uniform pay scales, retirement age and allowances.
- **Holding:** Review largely rejected. The Court reiterated that the service conditions of judicial officers should be prescribed by a separate Pay Commission set up exclusively for them, with uniform pay scales; financial burden is not a valid objection since the scales are the minimum deserved; some directions on allowances and transport were modified; time extended.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *7[iv]:* “Hence we reiterate the importance of such separate commission and also of the desirability of prescribing uniform pay scales to the judges all over the country. Since such pay scales will be the minimum deserved by the judicial officers, the argument that some of the States may not be able to bear the financial burden is irrelevant. The uniform service conditions as and when laid down would not, of course, affect any special or extra benefits which some States may be bestowing upon their judicial officers.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Authority that judicial officers' pay is to be set by a separate judicial pay commission and that State financial constraints do not defeat the minimum scales.

## State of Haryana v. O.P. Gupta (1996-01-12)
- **File:** `SC_1996-01-12_State-of-Haryana-v-OP-Gupta.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; (see citation)
- **Citation:** JT 1996 (3) SC 141; AIR 1996 SC 2936
- **Bench:** K. Ramaswamy, G.B. Pattanaik, JJ.
- **Topic:** Notional promotion / arrears
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1388703/
- **Facts:** Engineers were given promotion with a deemed date (01.01.1983) after the seniority list was re-prepared under Rule 9 on this Court's direction, but joined promotional posts on 01.12.1992; the High Court allowed arrears for the period.
- **Holding:** Arrears not payable for the period of deemed promotion in which the higher post was not worked ('no work no pay'); the employee is entitled only to step up of pay notionally from the deemed date. Jankiraman distinguished.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *(unnumbered):* “This Court in Paluru Ramakrishnaiah & Ors. vs. Union of India & Anr. [(1989) 2 SCR 92 at page 109] considered the direction issued by the High Court and upheld that there has to be "no pay for no work", i.e., a person will not be entitled to any pay and allowance during the period for which he did not perform the duties of higher post, although after due consideration, he was given a proper place in the gradation list having been deemed to be promoted to the higher post with effect from the date his junior was promoted. He will be entitled only to step up the scale of pay retrospectively from the deemed date but is not entitled to the payment of arrears of the salary.”
  - *(unnumbered):* “In these appeals unless the seniority list is prepared and finalised and promotions are made in accordance with the Rules on the basis of the above seniority list, the question of entitlement to work in the promotional posts does not arise. Consequently, the payment of arrears of salary does not arise since, admittedly the respondents had not worked during that period. The High Court was, therefore, wholly illegal in directing payment of arrears of salary. The order of the High Court accordingly is quashed.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Cite where notional (deemed date) promotion is given on seniority revision and arrears of the higher pay are claimed for the period not worked.

## Selvaraj v. Lt. Governor of Island, Port Blair (1998-03-16)
- **File:** `SC_1998-03-16_Selvaraj-v-Lt-Governor-Island.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; not stated in source header
- **Citation:** (1998) 4 SCC 291; AIR 1999 SC 838
- **Bench:** S.B. Majmudar, S.P. Kurdukar, JJ.
- **Topic:** Officiating / additional charge pay
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/83314/
- **Facts:** A primary school teacher was ordered to look after the duties of Secretary (Scouts), with pay to be drawn against that post, but was not paid the higher scale for 29.01.1992 to 19.09.1995.
- **Holding:** Having actually worked on the higher post, though temporarily and in an officiating capacity, he is entitled on quantum meruit to the difference of salary of the higher scale for the period worked; this does not amount to promotion.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *3:* “Fact remains that the appellant has worked on the higher post though temporarily and in an officiating capacity pursuant to the aforesaid order and his salary was to be drawn during that time against the post of Secretary (Scouts). It is also not in dispute that the salary attached to the post of Secretary (Scouts) was in the pay scale of 1640-2900. Consequently, on the principle of quantum meruit the respondents authorities should have paid the appellant as per the emoluments available in the aforesaid higher pay scale during the time he actually worked on the said post of Secretary (Scouts) though in an officiating capacity and not as a regular promotee. This limited relief is required to be given to the appellant only on this ground.”
  - *4:* “It is made clear that the payment of the aforesaid difference amount of salary shall not be treated to amount to any promotion given to the appellant on the said post. It is only on the ground that he had actually worked, as such this relief is being given to him.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Cite for pay difference to an employee who actually performed duties of a higher post under an order, without any claim to promotion.

## Secretary-cum-Chief Engineer, Chandigarh v. Hari Om Sharma (1998-04-29)
- **File:** `SC_1998-04-29_Chief-Engineer-Chandigarh-v-Hari-Om-Sharma.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; not stated in source header
- **Citation:** (1998) 5 SCC 87; AIR 1998 SC 2909
- **Bench:** S. Saghir Ahmad, K. Venkataswami, S. Rajendra Babu, JJ.
- **Topic:** Officiating pay / stop-gap promotion
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1949929/
- **Facts:** Respondent was put to officiate as Junior Engineer-I in a stop-gap arrangement from 1990 and continued but was not paid the salary of that post. The Tribunal ordered payment of salary and consideration for regular promotion.
- **Holding:** Appeal dismissed. A person put to officiate on a higher post with greater responsibilities is normally entitled to the salary of that post; an undertaking not to claim benefits of a stop-gap higher post is contrary to law and public policy (Section 23, Contract Act).
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *6:* “If a person is put to officiate on a higher post with greater responsibilities, he is normally entitled to salary of that post. The Tribunal has noticed that the respondent has been working on the post of Junior Engineer-I since 1990 and promotion for such a long period of time cannot be treated to be a stop-gap arrangement.”
  - *8:* “An agreement that if a person is promoted to the higher post or put to officiate on that post or, as in instant case, a stop-gap arrangement is made to place him on the higher post, he would not claim higher salary or other attendant benefits would be contrary to law and also against public policy . it would, therefore, be unenforceable in view of Section 23 of the Contract Act.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Cite for salary of the higher post to an employee made to officiate for a long period, and against any undertaking waiving that pay.

## State of Haryana v. Tilak Raj (2003-07-14)
- **File:** `SC_2003-07-14_State-of-Haryana-v-Tilak-Raj.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; (see citation)
- **Citation:** AIR 2003 SC 2658
- **Bench:** Doraiswamy Raju, Arijit Pasayat, JJ.
- **Topic:** Pay parity (daily wagers)
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1219437/
- **Facts:** Thirty-five helpers engaged on daily wages in Haryana Roadways claimed regularisation or the pay of regular employees; the High Court directed minimum of the regular pay scale with dearness allowance.
- **Holding:** High Court direction set aside. A scale of pay is attached to a post and a daily wager holds no post; equal pay for equal work needs complete and wholesale identity; daily wagers are entitled to the prescribed minimum wages, if more than what is paid.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *(unnumbered):* “A scale of pay is attached to a definite post and in case of a daily wager, he holds no posts. The respondent workers cannot be held to hold any posts to claim even any comparison with the regular and permanent staff for any or all purposes including a claim for equal pay and allowances. To claim a relief on the basis of equality, it is for the claimants to substantiate a clear cut basis of equivalence and a resultant hostile discrimination before becoming eligible to claim rights on a par with the other group vis- à-vis an alleged discrimination. No material was placed before the High Court as to the nature of the duties of either categories and it is not possible to hold that the principle of "equal pay for equal work" is an abstract one. "Equal pay for equal work" is a concept which requires for its applicability complete and wholesale identity between a group of employees claiming identical pay scales and the other group of employees who have already earned such pay scales. The problem about equal pay cannot always be translated into a mathematical formula. Judged in the background of aforesaid legal principles, the impugned judgment of the High Court is clearly indefensible and the same is set aside.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Cite to distinguish pay claims of daily-rated/non-post holders from regular employees (read with Jagjit Singh, 2016).

## State of Haryana v. Charanjit Singh (2005-10-05)
- **File:** `SC_2005-10-05_State-of-Haryana-v-Charanjit-Singh.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; (see citation)
- **Citation:** (2006) 9 SCC 321; AIR 2006 SC 161
- **Bench:** S.N. Variava, Ar. Lakshmanan, S.H. Kapadia, JJ.
- **Topic:** Pay parity
- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1330629/
- **Facts:** Batch of appeals by State of Haryana against High Court decisions granting equal pay to daily-rated, casual and contract workers following a Full Bench decision.
- **Holding:** Jasmer Singh, Tilak Raj, Orissa University of Agriculture and Tarun K. Roy lay down the correct law: equal pay must be for equal work of equal value; the burden of proof lies on the claimant; the doctrine is not available to contract employees; matters remitted to the High Court.
- **Key paragraphs (verbatim):**
  - *17:* “Undoubtedly, the doctrine of "equal pay for equal work" is not an abstract doctrine and is capable of being enforced in a Court of law. But equal pay must be for equal work of equal value. The principle of "equal pay for equal work" has no mechanical application in every case. Article 14 permits reasonable classification based on qualities or characteristics of persons recruited and grouped together, as against those who were left out. Of course, the qualities or characteristics must have a reasonable relation to the object sought to be achieved. In service matters, merit or experience can be a proper basis for classification for the purposes of pay in order to promote efficiency in administration.”
- **Use in Desk-6 notes:** Cite for burden of proof and tests (equal work, equal quality, same conditions) in equal pay claims.

## Secretary, State of Karnataka v. Umadevi (3) (2006-04-10)
- **File:** `SC_2006-04-10_Umadevi.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Supreme Court of India; (see citation)
- **Citation:** (2006) 4 SCC 1; AIR 2006 SC 1806

- **Topic:** Regularisation

- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/1100983/

- **File:** `BHC_2024-04-12_Sunanda-Sonawane-v-District-Judge-Dhule.txt`
- **Court / case no.:** Bombay High Court; Writ Petition No. 2394 of 2015 (Bombay High Court, Aurangabad Bench, Full Bench), judgment dated 12.04.2024
- **Citation:** Writ Petition No. 2394 of 2015 (Bombay High Court, Aurangabad Bench, Full Bench), judgment dated 12.04.2024
- **Bench:** Ravindra V. Ghuge, R.G. Avachat, Sandipkumar C. More, JJ. (Full Bench)

- **Source:** https://indiankanoon.org/doc/124267874/

At the stroke of completion of 12 years, he has to be scrutinised and found to be eligible. He can then be divested of the benefits of the first or the second benefits, which suffering is his own creation.”

If at those stages, he is held eligible, the ACPS benefits would be extended to him. Once a candidate is held eligible for the 1st ACPS monetary benefits, the said benefits cannot be withdrawn for subsequently having been found ineligible at the time of the de-facto promotion. As like the examples cited above, declaring the person ineligible with retrospective effect or deprive him of the benefits which he has qualified to earn earlier, is irrational, illogical and unjustified. Therefore, for not reaching the benchmark, stoppage of the benefits, already extended after due compliance of clause 2(5) of the 2001 GR at the stage of completion of 12 years, is impermissible. This would not be applicable to a case of disqualification, viz. when a candidate refuses to accept the actual promotional post when selected and declines to shoulder the responsibilities of the said post.”
